# Alabama Current Use Valuation for Beekeeping - Complete Research

**Research Date:** February 9, 2026  
**Purpose:** Build Alabama beekeeping agricultural exemption calculator

---

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: Code of Alabama §40-7-25.1 through §40-7-25.3
**Also known as:** "Current Use Valuation" (not technically an "exemption" — it's an alternative valuation method)

#### Key Law:
- **§40-7-25.1** — Establishes current use valuation for agricultural, forest, and residential property (Class III)
- **§40-7-25.2** — Application procedures and qualifications
- **§40-7-25.3** — Rollback provisions when property is converted

#### History:
- **1978:** Legislature passed original current use valuation act
- **1982:** Amended to include definitive formula for calculating current use values
- Alabama Department of Revenue (ALDOR) calculates and publishes current use values annually

### Administrative Rule: 810-4-1-.01
Alabama Department of Revenue's Current Use Valuation regulations provide detailed implementation guidance.

---

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Definition of Agricultural Property
Per §40-7-25.1, agricultural property includes:
> "All real property used for raising, harvesting, and selling crops or for the feeding, breeding, management, raising, sale of, or the production of livestock"

**✅ Beekeeping qualifies as agricultural use in Alabama** — bees are classified as livestock under Alabama agricultural law, and honey/beeswax production constitutes "raising, harvesting, and selling crops" and "production of livestock."

### Requirements:
1. Property must be **Class III** (agricultural, forest, or residential)
2. Must be actively used for agricultural purposes
3. The assessor evaluates based on **actual current use** on October 1 of the tax year
4. No specific minimum hive count set by state law
5. Must demonstrate **bona fide agricultural activity** — not hobby or incidental

### Practical Guidance:
- Most county assessors look for evidence of commercial agricultural activity
- Having a farm plan, sales records, or registration with the Alabama Department of Agriculture helps
- Beekeepers should register hives with the Alabama Department of Agriculture and Industries (ADAI)

---

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State Law:
- **No strict minimum acreage** for current use valuation
- However, on **parcels of 5 acres or less**, the assessing official may require submission of additional data (such as site management plans)
- In practice, properties under 5 acres face more scrutiny

### County Variation:
- Each county assessor has discretion in evaluating applications
- Larger parcels (10+ acres) are generally approved more easily
- Smaller parcels need stronger evidence of agricultural production

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Requirements:
- **No minimum hive count specified in state law**
- Must demonstrate "bona fide" agricultural use
- County assessors evaluate on case-by-case basis

### Practical Minimums:
- Most assessors expect to see evidence of genuine agricultural activity
- **Recommended: 6-8 hives minimum** for most counties to demonstrate bona fide beekeeping operation
- Having a bee inspection certificate from ADAI strengthens applications
- Some counties may accept fewer hives if combined with other agricultural activities

---

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Filing Period:
- **October 1 through January 1** of the applicable tax year
- Property use is evaluated as of **October 1** (the lien date in Alabama)

### Where to Apply:
- Filed with the **County Tax Assessor** (or Revenue Commissioner in some counties)
- Application requires:
  - Description of property
  - General description of agricultural use
  - May require aerial photographs (especially for forest property)
  - Assessor may request additional documentation

### Initial vs. Renewal:
- **Initial application required** when first seeking current use valuation
- **No annual renewal required** — once granted, valuation continues
- **New owner must reapply** between October 1 and January 1 after purchase; otherwise, property reverts to market value

### Documentation Recommended:
- Farm plan or business plan
- Hive registration with ADAI
- Income/expense records from beekeeping
- Honey sales receipts
- Bee inspection certificates

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How It Works:
Alabama property is assessed at different ratios based on class:
- **Class I:** Utilities — 30% assessment ratio
- **Class II:** Non-owner-occupied property — 20% assessment ratio  
- **Class III:** Agricultural, forest, residential, historic — **10% assessment ratio**
- **Class IV:** Private motor vehicles/property — varies

### Current Use vs. Market Value:
- **Market value:** Estimated price at fair voluntary sale
- **Current use value:** Value based on actual agricultural use (not potential development value)
- ALDOR publishes annual current use values per acre by type

### Typical Current Use Values (approximate):
- **Row cropland:** $350-$600/acre
- **Pastureland:** $200-$400/acre  
- **Timberland:** $250-$500/acre
- **Market value** in many areas: $3,000-$15,000+/acre

### Example Savings:
| Factor | Market Value | Current Use Value |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| Land value (10 acres) | $50,000 | $3,500 |
| Assessment (10%) | $5,000 | $350 |
| Tax rate (assume 5.0 mills) | $250/year | $17.50/year |
| **Annual savings** | — | **$232.50** |

*Note: Alabama has some of the lowest property tax rates in the nation*

### Assessment Ratio:
All Class III property (including agricultural) is assessed at **10% of appraised value** — this is already the lowest assessment ratio. Current use valuation further reduces the appraised value.

## 7. COUNTY STRUCTURE

### Overview:
- Alabama has **67 counties**
- Property tax administration varies by county:
  - Some counties have a **Tax Assessor** and separate **Revenue Commissioner**
  - Other counties combine these into a single **Revenue Commissioner** office
  - The **County Tax Assessor** handles property valuation and classification
  - The **Revenue Commissioner** handles tax collection

### Assessment Administration:
- **County Tax Assessor** — responsible for appraising property and determining current use qualification
- **County Revenue Commissioner** — collects taxes (in some counties, also handles assessment)
- **State involvement** — ALDOR publishes current use values annually; county assessors apply them

## 8. AVERAGE TAX RATES

### State Overview:
- Alabama has among the **lowest property taxes in the nation**
- **State average effective rate:** approximately 0.40% (40 mills on assessed value)
- Alabama's property tax is based on **mills** (1 mill = $0.001 per dollar of assessed value)

### Tax Rate Components:
- State tax: 6.5 mills
- County tax: varies (typically 10-30 mills)
- Municipal tax: varies (0-15 mills)
- School tax: varies (10-30 mills)

### Notable County Rates (approximate total millage):
- **Jefferson County (Birmingham):** ~78 mills
- **Madison County (Huntsville):** ~58 mills
- **Mobile County:** ~62 mills
- **Baldwin County:** ~42 mills
- **Shelby County:** ~55 mills
- **Rural counties:** 30-45 mills typical

*Note: Because assessed value is only 10% of appraised value for Class III, effective tax rates are much lower than millage suggests*

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### Alabama Climate & Beekeeping:
- **Climate zone:** Humid subtropical (USDA zones 7b-9a)
- **Nectar flow:** Primarily March through September
- **Major nectar sources:** Clover, wildflowers, tulip poplar, sourwood, cotton, soybeans, privet, gallberry (south)

### Average Production:
- **Average yield:** 50-70 lbs/hive/year
- **North Alabama:** 40-60 lbs/hive (shorter season, diverse flora)
- **Central Alabama:** 50-70 lbs/hive (good balance of flora)
- **South Alabama/Gulf Coast:** 60-80 lbs/hive (longer season, gallberry, tallow)

### Revenue Potential:
- Local honey: $8-$15/lb retail
- Wholesale: $4-$7/lb
- 10 hives × 60 lbs × $10/lb = **$6,000/year potential gross**

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Rollback Provisions (§40-7-25.3):
Two scenarios trigger rollback:

1. **Sale followed by conversion:** If property valued at current use is sold and the new owner converts it to non-qualifying use **within 2 years** of sale
2. **Direct conversion:** If property valued at current use is converted to non-qualifying use

### Rollback Calculation:
- Rollback covers the **3 tax years preceding** the October 1 following conversion
- The **sales price or market value, whichever is greater**, is used to calculate additional taxes due
- Assessment uses the **Class III ratio of 10%** (not Class II 20%)

### Key Details:
- If property was under current use for fewer than 3 years, rollback only covers actual years
- Rollback taxes are charged against the **owner of record on October 1** following conversion
- The person who caused the conversion may not be the person who pays if ownership changed

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Can You Have Both? **YES**

#### Alabama Homestead Exemption:
- Alabama offers various homestead exemptions under §40-9-19 through §40-9-21
- **Standard homestead exemption:** Exempts first $4,000 of assessed value from state tax
- **Over-65 exemption:** Additional exemptions available for seniors
- **Disability exemption:** Available for qualifying disabled homeowners

#### Interaction with Current Use:
- **Current use valuation and homestead exemption can be combined**
- Current use reduces the appraised value → homestead exemption further reduces assessed value
- The house/homesite is valued at market value (excluded from agricultural current use)
- Agricultural land around the home can receive current use valuation
- This creates a powerful combination for rural landowners

#### Practical Application:
1. Home + immediate yard = market value with homestead exemption
2. Agricultural land (including beehives) = current use valuation
3. Both benefits apply simultaneously

## ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Alabama-Specific Considerations:
1. **No state income tax on agricultural income under $50,000** (favorable for beekeepers)
2. **Property tax is extremely low** compared to other states — savings from current use may be modest in dollar terms but significant in percentage terms
3. **County variation is significant** — urban counties (Jefferson, Madison, Mobile) will show much larger savings than rural counties
4. **Alabama Forestry Commission** provides site management plans that can support applications for parcels under 5 acres
5. **ADAI registration** of apiaries is recommended but enforcement varies
