## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: Tennessee Code Annotated § 67-5-1001 through § 67-5-1032 — Agricultural, Forest, and Open Space Land Act (Greenbelt Law)

Tennessee's agricultural property tax program is commonly called the **Greenbelt Law**:

- **T.C.A. § 67-5-1001** — Short title
- **T.C.A. § 67-5-1002** — Legislative findings and purpose
- **T.C.A. § 67-5-1003** — Constitutional authorization
- **T.C.A. § 67-5-1004** — Definitions
- **T.C.A. § 67-5-1005** — Classification of agricultural land
- **T.C.A. § 67-5-1008** — Present use valuation; rollback taxes

### How Greenbelt Works
- Qualifying agricultural, forest, or open space land is assessed at its **present use value** rather than market value
- Property is assessed at **25% of appraised value** (farm classification) rather than **25% of market value** (residential/commercial)
- The present use value is based on the land's agricultural productivity
- If land use changes, **rollback taxes** for the difference in value are assessed for the **3 most recent years** prior to disqualification, plus the current year

### Constitutional Basis
- Tennessee Constitution, Article II, Section 28 authorizes the General Assembly to provide for classification and assessment of agricultural, forest, and open space land based on present use value

### Assessment Structure
- Tennessee assesses all property at **25% of appraised value** (assessment ratio)
- Farm property is assessed at **25% of present use value** (Greenbelt)
- Non-farm property is assessed at **25% of market value**
- Tax rates set at county level

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Does Beekeeping Qualify?

**YES** — Beekeeping qualifies as agricultural land use under Tennessee's Greenbelt Law.

**T.C.A. § 67-5-1004(1)** defines "Agricultural land" as land that:
> "Constitutes a farm unit engaged in the production or growing of agricultural products"

**T.C.A. § 1-3-105** defines "Agriculture" broadly as:
> "The land, buildings and machinery used in the commercial production of farm products and nursery stock"

Honey and bee products are recognized as agricultural products. The Tennessee Department of Agriculture oversees apiculture, and beekeeping is an established agricultural activity in the state.

### Key Requirements
1. Property must constitute a **farm unit** engaged in agricultural production
2. Must meet **minimum acreage requirements** (15 acres for a single tract)
3. Must show **gross agricultural income** of at least **$1,500/year average** over any 3-year period (creates rebuttable presumption)
4. Must be under a **sound management program**
5. Application filed with **County Assessor of Property**

### Income Threshold
**T.C.A. § 67-5-1005(a)(3):**
> "The assessor may presume that a tract of land is used as agricultural land, if the land produces gross agricultural income averaging at least $1,500 per year over any three-year period"

This $1,500/year threshold is important for beekeepers — must demonstrate income from honey sales, pollination services, or other bee products.

### Tennessee Beekeeping Regulation
- Tennessee Apiary Act: T.C.A. § 44-15-101 et seq.
- Register with TN Department of Agriculture, Plant Industries Division
- Annual inspection may be conducted
- Registration helps document agricultural use for Greenbelt

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State-Level Requirements (T.C.A. § 67-5-1004(1)(B))

**Agricultural Land must be one of:**
1. **Single tract of at least 15 acres** (including woodlands and wastelands)
2. **Two non-contiguous tracts in the same county**, one at least 15 acres and the other at least 10 acres, together constituting a farm unit
3. **Two non-contiguous tracts in the same county** totaling at least 15 acres, separated only by a road, body of water, or easement

### Important Notes
- The **15-acre minimum** is a state requirement — counties cannot reduce it
- Woodlands and wastelands count toward the acreage minimum
- The tracts must be in the **same county** if using the multi-tract option
- This is higher than Georgia (10 acres), Texas (5-20 varies), or Florida (no minimum)

### Alternative: Longtime Family Farm
T.C.A. § 67-5-1004(1)(A)(ii):
> Land that has been farmed by the owner or the owner's parent or spouse for at least **25 years** and is used as the residence of the owner

This alternative doesn't require commercial production but requires a long family history.

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Requirements
- Tennessee law does **NOT specify a minimum number of hives**
- The key threshold is the **$1,500/year gross agricultural income** (3-year average)
- County assessors determine whether the operation is bona fide

### Practical Requirements for Meeting the $1,500 Income Threshold
- At wholesale prices (~$3-5/lb), need ~300-500 lbs of honey/year minimum
- At retail prices (~$8-15/lb), need ~100-190 lbs of honey/year
- **Estimated minimum hives:** 5-10 hives to reliably produce enough income
- Additional income from: pollination services, wax, nucleus colonies, queen sales

### Typical Expectations by Acreage
- **15 acres (minimum):** 6-12 hives
- **20-30 acres:** 10-15 hives
- **50+ acres:** 15-25+ hives, or combination with other ag uses
- Combination with other agriculture (cattle, hay, timber) strengthens the application

### Documentation
- Keep detailed records of honey production and sales
- File Schedule F with federal tax return
- Register with TN Department of Agriculture
- Maintain a management plan

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Filing a Greenbelt Application

1. **Where to Apply:** County Assessor of Property
2. **Form:** Prescribed by the State Board of Equalization (GBL-1 or similar)
3. **Deadline:** Must be filed by **March 15** of the tax year
   - Late applications accepted within 30 days of disqualification notice with $50 fee
4. **Documentation Needed:**
   - Completed application form
   - Description of property and agricultural use
   - Acreage details (must meet 15-acre minimum)
   - Evidence of agricultural operation (beekeeping specifics)
   - Income documentation ($1,500/year average over 3 years)
   - Management plan
   - TN Department of Agriculture registration
5. **Review:** County Assessor determines eligibility

### Important Application Details
- **First-time applications** require detailed documentation
- **Reapplication not required** as long as ownership unchanged
- **Ownership changes** require new application by new owner
- Assessor sends notice of disqualification to owners who may qualify under new ownership
- **No formal covenant** (unlike Georgia) — classification continues until use changes

### Verification
- Assessor verifies agricultural use during on-site reviews (T.C.A. § 67-5-1005(c))
- May request proof of use or ownership at any time
- Income documentation may be requested periodically

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### Tennessee Property Tax Structure
- All property assessed at **25% of appraised value**
- Greenbelt: assessed at **25% of present use value**
- Tax rates expressed in dollars per $100 of assessed value
- State levy: $0.25 per $100 of assessed value (very small)

### Typical Reduction

**Present Use Value (Greenbelt) vs. Market Value:**
- Market value: $5,000-$50,000+/acre
- Greenbelt present use value: $250-$1,000/acre (based on soil productivity)
- **Savings: 80-98% reduction on land taxes**

**Example — 20 acres in Williamson County (Franklin, south of Nashville):**
- Market value: $100,000/acre × 20 = $2,000,000
- Assessed at 25%: $500,000
- Tax rate: $2.12 per $100 assessed
- Market value tax: $10,600/year

- Greenbelt value: $500/acre × 20 = $10,000
- Assessed at 25%: $2,500
- Tax rate: $2.12 per $100
- Greenbelt tax: $53/year
- **Annual savings: $10,547**

**Example — 25 acres in rural McMinn County (East TN):**
- Market value: $8,000/acre × 25 = $200,000
- Assessed at 25%: $50,000
- Tax rate: $2.78 per $100
- Market value tax: $1,390/year

- Greenbelt value: $350/acre × 25 = $8,750
- Assessed at 25%: $2,188
- Tax rate: $2.78 per $100
- Greenbelt tax: $61/year
- **Annual savings: $1,329**

### Savings by Region
- **Middle Tennessee (Nashville metro):** Highest savings due to rapidly appreciating land values
- **East Tennessee (Knoxville, Chattanooga):** Significant savings, especially in growing counties
- **West Tennessee (Memphis area):** Moderate savings, lower land values but still substantial

### Overview
- **Tennessee has 95 counties**
- Property assessment by **County Assessor of Property** (or Property Assessor)
- Tax collection by **County Trustee**
- State oversight by **Comptroller's Office, Division of Property Assessments**

### Key Officials
- **County Assessor of Property (Property Assessor)** — Determines values, reviews Greenbelt applications
- **County Trustee** — Collects property taxes
- **County Board of Equalization** — Handles appeals
- **State Board of Equalization** — Statewide appeals body

### State Oversight
- Tennessee Comptroller of the Treasury, Office of State Assessed Properties
- Division of Property Assessments sets guidelines for assessors
- Publishes present use value schedules for Greenbelt
- Ensures uniformity across counties

### County Regions
- **East Tennessee (33 counties):** Knoxville, Chattanooga, Smoky Mountains
- **Middle Tennessee (41 counties):** Nashville, Murfreesboro, Clarksville
- **West Tennessee (21 counties):** Memphis, Jackson

- **Middle Tennessee (Basin/Highland Rim):** USDA Zones 6b-7b; diverse nectar, clover, wildflowers

**Tennessee Beekeeping Industry:**
- Tennessee ranks in the top 20 states for beekeeping
- ~5,000+ beekeepers in the state
- Tennessee Beekeepers Association (TBA) very active with local chapters

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Greenbelt Rollback Taxes (T.C.A. § 67-5-1008)

**When Rollback is Triggered:**
- Land is converted to non-agricultural use
- Land is sold for non-agricultural purposes
- Owner fails to maintain agricultural classification
- New owner doesn't apply for Greenbelt within required timeframe

**Rollback Amount (T.C.A. § 67-5-1008(d)):**
- **3 most recent tax years** prior to disqualification PLUS the current year
- Rollback = difference between market value taxes and Greenbelt taxes for those years
- Plus **interest** from the date each year's taxes would have been due

**This is relatively moderate** compared to Georgia's full-covenant rollback with 20% penalty.

**Example — 20 acres in Williamson County, Greenbelt for 5 years:**
- Annual tax differential: ~$10,547
- 3-year + current year rollback: ~$42,188
- Interest (estimated): ~$3,000
- **Total rollback: ~$45,188**

**Exceptions (No Rollback):**
- Condemnation / eminent domain
- Property taken for public use
- Certain transfers to lineal descendants if they continue agricultural use
- Death of owner (heirs can continue classification)
- Involuntary destruction by natural disaster (if land still qualifies)

### Important Notes
- Rollback taxes become a lien on the property
- Assessor records the Greenbelt application with the register of deeds
- This provides notice to potential buyers of the Greenbelt classification
- Rollback taxes are the responsibility of the owner at time of disqualification

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Can You Have Both Greenbelt + Tax Relief?

**YES** — Tennessee has several property tax relief programs that can work with Greenbelt.

**Tennessee Property Tax Relief Programs:**

1. **Tax Relief for Elderly/Disabled (T.C.A. § 67-5-702):**
   - Age **65+** or permanently disabled
   - Income limits apply (adjusted annually, currently ~$31,280)
   - Reimburses property taxes up to a **maximum amount** (currently ~$300-500/year)
   - Does NOT reduce the assessed value — instead reimburses after payment
   - State-funded program through the Comptroller's Office

2. **Tax Relief for Disabled Veterans:**
   - 100% disabled veterans exempt from property taxes on primary residence
   - Up to certain value limits
   - Can be combined with Greenbelt on agricultural land

3. **Tax Freeze for Seniors (varies by county):**
   - Some counties offer a tax freeze for homeowners 65+
   - Freezes the tax amount at the year the freeze was approved
   - County-specific program, not statewide

**How They Interact:**
- Tax relief programs apply to the **residence**
- Greenbelt applies to the **agricultural land**
- Both can apply to the same property
- Residence is assessed at market value (less any relief)
- Agricultural land is assessed at present use value (Greenbelt)

**Tennessee does NOT have a traditional "homestead exemption"** like Florida or Georgia. Instead, it offers:
- Tax relief reimbursement for elderly/disabled
- Disabled veteran exemptions
- Some counties have local programs

**Optimal Strategy:**
1. Apply for Greenbelt on 15+ acres of agricultural land
2. If age 65+ or disabled: Apply for Tax Relief reimbursement on residence
3. If 100% disabled veteran: Apply for veteran exemption on residence
4. Maintain beekeeping operation meeting $1,500/year income threshold
5. Result: Minimized taxes on both residence and agricultural land

## 13. ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Tennessee-Specific Nuances

1. **15-Acre Minimum is Higher Than Most States:** This limits the program to genuinely rural or larger properties. Small suburban lots won't qualify.

2. **$1,500 Income Requirement:** This is a presumption, not an absolute requirement. But meeting it makes qualification much easier. Beekeepers should aim to exceed this threshold.

3. **No State Income Tax on Wages:** Tennessee relies heavily on property taxes and sales tax for local revenue. This means property tax rates can be relatively high compared to states with income taxes.

4. **Fast-Growing Metro Areas:** Nashville/Davidson, Williamson, and Rutherford counties are experiencing explosive growth. Land values are rising rapidly, making Greenbelt savings increasingly significant.

5. **Assessment Cycle:** Tennessee doesn't have a uniform reappraisal schedule like NC's octennial cycle. Counties reappraise on different schedules (typically 4-6 years). State board ensures compliance.

6. **Present Use Value Formula (T.C.A. § 67-5-1008(c)):**
   - Value = (2 × Use Value + Farm Land Value) ÷ 3
   - Use value based on agricultural income divided by capitalization rate
   - Farm land value based on farm-to-farm sales
   - Rate of increase capped at 6% per year since last reappraisal

7. **Recording Requirement:** The assessor may record the Greenbelt application with the register of deeds, providing public notice. If the assessor doesn't, the property owner must.
