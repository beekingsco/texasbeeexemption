**Research Date:** February 9, 2026
**Purpose:** Build North Carolina beekeeping agricultural exemption calculator

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: N.C. General Statutes § 105-277.2 through § 105-277.7 — Present-Use Value Program (PUV)

North Carolina's agricultural property tax program is called **Present-Use Value (PUV)**:

- **N.C.G.S. § 105-277.2** — Definitions
- **N.C.G.S. § 105-277.3** — Agricultural, horticultural, and forestland
- **N.C.G.S. § 105-277.4** — Application and qualification
- **N.C.G.S. § 105-277.5** — Changes in use; rollback taxes
- **N.C.G.S. § 105-277.6** — Use-value advisory board
- **N.C.G.S. § 105-277.7** — Deferred taxes

### How PUV Works
- Qualifying agricultural, horticultural, or forestry land is taxed at its **present-use value** (productive agricultural value) rather than **market value**
- The difference between market value tax and PUV tax is **deferred** (not eliminated)
- If land use changes, deferred taxes for the most recent **3 years** become due (rollback)
- PUV is NOT an exemption — it's a **tax deferral program**

### Constitutional Basis
- North Carolina Constitution, Article V, Section 2(2) authorizes the General Assembly to classify agricultural, horticultural, and forestland for taxation based on present-use value

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Does Beekeeping Qualify?

**YES** — Beekeeping qualifies as an agricultural operation under NC PUV.

**N.C.G.S. § 105-277.2** defines "agricultural land" as:
> Land that is a part of a farm unit that is actively engaged in the commercial production or growing of crops, plants, or animals under a sound management program. The term includes... the production of **animals** useful to man...

Bees are animals, and honey/bee products are agricultural products. North Carolina General Statutes explicitly include animal husbandry in the definition of agriculture. The NC Department of Revenue's Present-Use Value Guide includes livestock and animal operations as qualifying uses.

### Key Requirements
1. Property must be part of a **farm unit** that produces agricultural products
2. Must be under a **sound management program**
3. Must demonstrate **commercial production** — hobby operations may not qualify
4. Income must be derived from the agricultural operation
5. Owner must personally manage or oversee the operation (or lease to someone who does)

### NC Beekeeping Registration
- North Carolina Department of Agriculture & Consumer Services regulates beekeeping
- N.C.G.S. Chapter 106, Article 55 — Bee and Honey Act
- Registration with the NC Plant Industry Division, Apiary Section
- Annual registration fee is minimal
- Helps document agricultural use for PUV application

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State-Level Requirements

**For Agricultural Land (N.C.G.S. § 105-277.3):**
- Minimum **10 acres** actively engaged in commercial agricultural production
- Must be part of a "farm unit"

**For Horticultural Land:**
- Minimum **5 acres** actively engaged in horticultural use

**For Forestland:**
- Minimum **20 acres** of forested land under a written forest management plan

### Which Category for Beekeeping?
- Beekeeping generally falls under **agricultural land** (10-acre minimum)
- If combined with horticultural uses (orchards, nurseries, etc.), may qualify under horticultural (5-acre minimum)
- County tax assessors determine the appropriate classification

### County Variations
- Counties follow the state minimums
- The county tax assessor determines if land qualifies
- Sound management program documentation is critical

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Requirements
- NC law does **NOT specify a minimum number of hives**
- The requirement is "commercial production" of agricultural products
- Must demonstrate a **sound management program**

### Practical Requirements
- **10 acres:** Generally 6-12 hives expected as evidence of bona fide agriculture
- **20+ acres:** 10-20+ hives, or combination with other agricultural uses
- Must demonstrate genuine commercial intent
- **Income requirement:** Property should produce agricultural income, though no specific dollar threshold is set by statute
- County assessors look at the totality of the operation

### Documentation Best Practices
- Register with NC Department of Agriculture, Apiary Inspection Section
- Maintain records of honey production and sales
- Keep management logs (inspections, treatments, feeding)
- File Schedule F with federal tax return showing farm income
- Have a written bee management plan

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Filing a PUV Application

1. **Where to Apply:** County Tax Assessor's Office (or Tax Administrator)
2. **Form:** Application for Present-Use Value Assessment (AV-5 or county-specific form)
3. **Deadline:** Must be filed by **June 1** for the current tax year
   - New owners must apply within 60 days of acquisition
4. **Documentation Needed:**
   - Completed application form
   - Description of property and agricultural use
   - Acreage breakdown (agricultural, homesite, waste, etc.)
   - Evidence of agricultural operation (beekeeping details)
   - Income documentation (sales records, tax returns)
   - Management plan
   - NC Department of Agriculture apiary registration
5. **Review:** County Tax Assessor reviews and determines eligibility

### Important Application Details
- **First-time applications** require detailed documentation
- **Ownership changes** require new application within 60 days
- **Continued qualification** — County assessor may verify use at any time
- **No formal covenant** required (unlike Georgia's 10-year covenant)
- **Annual listing** — Property owners should list their property annually (January 1 - January 31)

### Renewal
- Once approved, PUV continues as long as land remains in qualifying use
- No annual reapplication required unless ownership changes
- County assessor may request verification of continued use
- New octennial reappraisal may require updated documentation

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### Typical Reduction

NC taxes property at **100% of appraised value** (no assessment ratio like Georgia's 40%).

**PUV Assessment:**
- Market value: $5,000-$50,000+/acre depending on location
- Present-use value: $300-$1,500/acre (based on soil productivity class and use type)
- **Savings: 75-98% reduction on land taxes**

**Example — 15 acres in Wake County (Raleigh area):**
- Market value: $80,000/acre × 15 = $1,200,000
- Tax rate: $0.60 per $100 (county) + $0.20 per $100 (fire district) = $0.80
- Market value tax: $9,600/year

- PUV value: $800/acre × 15 = $12,000
- PUV tax: $96/year
- **Annual savings: $9,504**
- **Deferred taxes: $9,504/year accumulate (3-year rolling)**

**Example — 20 acres in rural Sampson County:**
- Market value: $5,000/acre × 20 = $100,000
- Tax rate: $0.78 per $100
- Market value tax: $780/year

- PUV value: $500/acre × 20 = $10,000
- PUV tax: $78/year
- **Annual savings: $702**

### Understanding Deferred Taxes
- **Critical distinction from other states:** NC PUV creates **deferred taxes**, not eliminated taxes
- The difference between market value tax and PUV tax accumulates as a lien on the property
- If the property is removed from agricultural use or sold for non-agricultural purposes, the **most recent 3 years of deferred taxes** become due, plus interest
- While in qualifying use, deferred taxes are not payable
- Only 3 years of rollback (not the full history) — much less punitive than Georgia's full-covenant rollback

## 7. COUNTY STRUCTURE

### Overview
- **North Carolina has 100 counties**
- Property assessment by **County Tax Assessor** (or Tax Administrator)
- Tax collection by **County Tax Collector** (sometimes combined with assessor)
- Each county's tax assessor reviews PUV applications

### Key Officials
- **County Tax Assessor / Tax Administrator** — Determines property values, reviews PUV applications
- **County Tax Collector** — Collects property taxes
- **Board of Equalization and Review** — Handles appeals (meets annually)

### NC Use-Value Advisory Board
- Statewide board that advises the NC Department of Revenue on present-use values
- Publishes **use-value schedules** for agricultural, horticultural, and forest land
- Values based on soil types and productivity classes
- Counties required to use the state's use-value manual

### Octennial Reappraisal
- NC counties must reappraise all property at least every **8 years** (octennial)
- Some counties reappraise more frequently (4-year cycle common)
- PUV values updated with each reappraisal
- State use-value schedule provides the agricultural values

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### North Carolina Climate & Beekeeping

**NC Climate Zones:**
- **Mountains (West):** USDA Zones 5b-7a; shorter season, excellent sourwood and wildflower honey
- **Piedmont (Central):** USDA Zones 7a-7b; good year-round potential, multiple nectar flows
- **Coastal Plain (East):** USDA Zones 7b-8b; long season, gallberry, tupelo, wildflower

**Average Honey Production:**
- **Mountains:** 35-55 lbs/hive/year (shorter season but premium honey)
- **Piedmont:** 50-75 lbs/hive/year
- **Coastal Plain:** 60-90 lbs/hive/year
- **State average:** ~50-65 lbs/hive/year

**Major Nectar Sources in NC:**
- Spring: Tulip poplar, black locust, redbud, fruit trees, clover
- Summer: Sourwood (Mountains — premium, $15-25/lb), basswood, wildflowers
- Fall: Goldenrod, aster
- Coastal: Gallberry (excellent honey), titi

**NC Beekeeping Industry:**
- North Carolina ranks in the top 15 states for beekeeping
- ~12,000+ registered beekeepers
- North Carolina State Beekeepers Association (NCSBA) very active
- NC State University has a strong apiculture research program
- **Sourwood honey** from Western NC is among the most valuable US honeys

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Change in Use — Deferred Tax Rollback (N.C.G.S. § 105-277.4)

**When Rollback is Triggered:**
- Property is voluntarily removed from agricultural use
- Property is sold for non-agricultural purposes
- Property no longer qualifies for PUV
- Failure to maintain sound management program

**Rollback Amount:**
- **3 most recent years** of deferred taxes become due
- Plus **interest** at the rate specified by law (currently around 5-7%)
- Only the difference between market value tax and PUV tax for those 3 years

**This is much less punitive than Georgia's full-covenant rollback.**

**Example — 15 acres in Wake County, PUV for 5 years:**
- Annual deferred taxes: ~$9,504
- 3-year rollback: ~$28,512
- Interest (estimated): ~$2,000
- **Total rollback: ~$30,512**

**Exceptions (No Rollback):**
- Condemnation / eminent domain
- Property transfer to government entity
- Certain transfers between qualifying family members (N.C.G.S. § 105-277.3(d))
- Owner death (heirs can continue PUV if they maintain agricultural use)
- Transfer to conservation organization for permanent conservation

### Important Notes
- Deferred taxes are a **lien on the property** and are recorded
- Potential buyers can see the deferred tax amount
- Title companies typically address deferred taxes at closing
- Rollback taxes are the responsibility of the owner at the time of disqualification

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Can You Have Both Homestead + PUV?

**YES** — North Carolina allows both a homestead exemption and PUV on the same property.

**NC Homestead Exemption (N.C.G.S. § 105-277.1):**
- Available to owners age **65+** or those who are **permanently and totally disabled**
- **Income limit:** Not to exceed $36,500 (adjusted annually)
- Excludes the **greater of $25,000 or 50% of appraised value** from taxation
- Must be owner-occupied primary residence

**How They Interact:**
- Homestead exemption applies to the **residence and surrounding land**
- PUV applies to the **agricultural acreage**
- Both can apply to the same property
- Homestead reduces taxes on the residential portion
- PUV defers taxes on the agricultural portion

**Key Difference from Other States:**
- NC's homestead exemption is **limited to age 65+ or disabled** — not available to all homeowners
- Most states (FL, GA) offer homestead to all owner-occupants regardless of age
- NC "homestead" is really a **senior/disability property tax relief program**

**Optimal Strategy for NC:**
1. If age 65+ or disabled: Apply for homestead exemption on residence
2. Apply for PUV on agricultural land (10+ acres)
3. Maintain beekeeping operation with proper documentation
4. Result: Maximum tax reduction on entire property

## 13. ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS

### NC-Specific Nuances

1. **Deferred Tax vs. Eliminated Tax:** NC PUV creates deferred taxes — they're suspended, not forgiven. This is a critical distinction for property buyers and sellers.

2. **Use-Value Manual:** NC publishes a statewide Use-Value Manual that county assessors MUST follow. This provides consistency but limits county discretion.

3. **Soil Productivity Classes:** NC bases agricultural values on USDA soil surveys. Better soils = slightly higher PUV values. Values range from ~$300-1,500/acre depending on soil class and use type.

4. **Octennial Reappraisal:** Market values (and thus the gap between market and PUV) are updated every 4-8 years. In fast-growing areas (Charlotte, Raleigh, Asheville), this can significantly increase savings.

5. **No Formal Covenant:** Unlike Georgia's 10-year covenant, NC PUV is more flexible. No penalty for simply selling to someone who continues agricultural use.

6. **Revenue-Neutral Tax Rate:** When counties do reappraisals, they must adopt a revenue-neutral tax rate. Market value increases don't automatically mean higher taxes.
