# Phone Research Guide - State Agricultural Exemptions for Beekeeping

**Purpose:** Efficiently gather accurate data on beekeeping property tax exemptions across all 50 states  
**Target:** State revenue departments, county assessors, extension offices, beekeeping associations  
**Estimated Time:** 15-30 minutes per state (including follow-up)

---

## BEFORE YOU CALL

### Preparation Checklist
- [ ] Have state name and current statute reference ready (from main analysis doc)
- [ ] Open web browser to state revenue/extension site
- [ ] Have note-taking template open (see below)
- [ ] Check time zone (call during business hours)
- [ ] Have callback number ready to provide

### Best Times to Call
- **State Revenue Departments:** Tuesday-Thursday, 10am-3pm local time
- **Extension Offices:** Monday-Friday, 9am-4pm local time  
- **County Assessors:** Tuesday-Thursday, 10am-2pm local time (avoid Monday = busy, Friday = early closures)

### Who to Ask For
1. **State Revenue/Taxation Department:**
   - "Property tax division"
   - "Agricultural assessment specialist"
   - "Exemptions coordinator"

2. **University Extension:**
   - "Agricultural economics specialist"
   - "Property tax specialist"
   - "Beekeeping/apiculture extension agent"

3. **County Assessor:**
   - "Chief appraiser" or "Chief assessor"
   - "Agricultural specialist"
   - Ask for a county with active beekeeping/agriculture

## CALL SCRIPT: State Revenue Department

### Opening
> "Hi, I'm researching agricultural property tax exemptions across the US and I have a few quick questions about [STATE]'s program. Do you have a moment, or can you direct me to someone who handles agricultural assessment questions?"

*[If transferred, repeat intro]*

### Core Questions (adjust based on responses)

**Q1: Agricultural Use Program**
> "Does [STATE] have an agricultural use valuation or agricultural assessment program for property taxes?"

- ✅ YES → Continue to Q2
- ❌ NO → "Are there any other property tax relief programs for agricultural land?" → Note response and move to "If No Program" section below
- 🤷 UNSURE → "Who would know about farmland property tax programs?" → Get referral

**Q2: Beekeeping Qualification**
> "Specifically, does beekeeping or apiculture qualify as an agricultural use under this program?"

- ✅ **"Yes"** → Continue to Q3
- 🤔 **"I think so" or "It should"** → "Can you point me to documentation?" Continue to Q3
- ❓ **"I'm not sure"** → "Would that be defined in the statute, or is there a list of qualifying activities?"
- ❌ **"No"** → "Is there a specific list of what qualifies? Is there an appeals or petition process?"

**Q3: Basic Requirements**
> "What are the basic eligibility requirements? I'm specifically interested in:"
> - Minimum acreage  
> - Minimum production requirements (like hive counts)
> - Income thresholds, if any
> - Application deadlines

*[Take detailed notes]*

**Q4: Administration**
> "Is this program administered at the state level or by counties? If county, does the process vary significantly between counties?"

**Q5: Resources**
> "Is there a handbook, application form, or webpage you can direct me to? Can I email you to request documentation?"

*[Get email address if willing to send materials]*

### Closing
> "Thank you so much. One last question - is there a county you'd recommend I call that has active beekeeping exemptions? Or an extension specialist who works with beekeepers on this?"

*[Get specific referral if possible]*

> "May I call you back if I have follow-up questions after reviewing the materials?"

*[Get direct number if available]*

**Q5: Local Contacts**

### Closing
> "This is really helpful. Would it be okay if I sent you a follow-up email with specific questions after I review what you've told me?"

## DIFFICULT SCENARIOS

### Scenario: "I'm not sure, you'd have to apply and see"

**Response:** 
> "I understand. Is there someone at the state level who interprets the statute? Or can you point me to the specific code section that defines agricultural use? I'm trying to help potential applicants understand their chances before they invest time in the process."

**Alternative:**
> "Have you had any similar cases - like hobby farms, orchards, or other small-scale agriculture? How were those treated?"

### Scenario: "That's a county decision"

**Response:**
> "I understand it's administered locally. Is there state-level guidance on what constitutes agricultural use, or is it entirely up to each county? Can you recommend 2-3 counties that are agricultural and might have processed applications like this?"

*[Get specific county names, then call them]*

### Scenario: "Call your local assessor"

**Response:**
> "I will definitely do that. Before I go, is there any state-level documentation - like a statute number or handbook - I can reference when I call them?"

*[Get whatever state-level info exists, then call assessor with that reference]*

### Scenario: "You need a lawyer/CPA"

**Response:**
> "I appreciate that, and I'll definitely recommend professional guidance. I'm just trying to understand if it's even possible under state law before someone invests in legal help. Do you know if anyone has successfully done this in [STATE]?"

### Scenario: "That information isn't public"

**Response:**
> "I'm not asking about specific taxpayers or parcels. I'm just trying to understand the general eligibility requirements that would be in the state statute or regulations. Those are public records, right? Can you point me to where those are published?"

## TIPS FOR EFFICIENT RESEARCH

1. **Batch by time zone** - Call East Coast states in morning, West Coast in afternoon
2. **Start with extension offices** - They're often friendlier and more informative than bureaucrats
3. **Use previous success as reference** - "In Texas, beekeeping qualifies under their ag use valuation. Does [STATE] have something similar?"
4. **Get specific county referrals** - Don't just call random counties; ask state-level contacts which counties to call
5. **Record (if legal)** - Many states are one-party consent. Recording ensures accuracy. Always ask: "Do you mind if I record this call for note-taking purposes?"
6. **Follow the referrals** - If someone says "Call Jane at Extension," DO IT immediately while it's fresh
7. **Be friendly but persistent** - Bureaucrats often say "I don't know" to end calls. Ask "Who would know?" until you find the right person

## RED FLAGS (Possible TIER 4)

Watch for these indicators that a state won't work:

- ❌ "We don't have any agricultural programs"
- ❌ "Beekeeping wouldn't qualify - that's not farming"
- ❌ "Only row crops and livestock qualify"
- ❌ "You'd need 100+ acres minimum" (for beekeeping, probably not viable)
- ❌ "We've never had a beekeeping application approved"
- ❌ Explicitly listed on exclusion list

Document these clearly but don't spend excessive time trying to force a square peg into a round hole.

---

## GREEN FLAGS (Possible TIER 1-2)

Indicators of a good state:

- ✅ "Yes, apiculture is explicitly listed"
- ✅ "We've processed several beekeeping applications"
- ✅ "Under our broad definition of agriculture, that would qualify"
- ✅ Low minimum acreage (5-20 acres)
- ✅ Clear application process with helpful staff
- ✅ Good extension resources and support
- ✅ Active beekeeping community using the program

These are your launch targets!

---

**Final Note:** Be professional, courteous, and respectful of these government employees' time. They're not obligated to do your research for you, so express genuine appreciation when they help. Building good relationships with state contacts can pay dividends when clients have questions later.
