## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: California Government Code §51200-51297.4 — The Williamson Act (California Land Conservation Act of 1965)
**Source:** [Government Code §51200](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=51200.&lawCode=GOV)

The **Williamson Act (CLCA)** is California's primary agricultural land preservation and tax benefit program. It enables local governments to enter into contracts with private landowners restricting land to agricultural or open-space use in exchange for reduced property tax assessments.

### Key Code Sections:
- **Gov. Code §51200** — Short title (Williamson Act / CLCA)
- **Gov. Code §51201** — Definitions (agricultural commodity, agricultural use, prime agricultural land)
- **Gov. Code §51230-51239** — Agricultural Preserves
- **Gov. Code §51240-51255** — Contracts (Land Conservation Contracts)
- **Gov. Code §51280-51287** — Cancellation of Contracts
- **Gov. Code §51290-51295** — Nonrenewal
- **Revenue & Taxation Code §423** — Valuation of Williamson Act lands

### Related Programs:
- **Farmland Security Zone (FSZ)** — Enhanced version with 20-year contracts and greater tax reduction (Gov. Code §51296-51297.4)
- **Revenue & Taxation Code §402.1** — Assessment of agricultural land generally
- **California Constitution Art. XIII §8** — Permits valuation of open-space land on use basis

### Administering Agency:
- **California Department of Conservation** — Division of Land Resource Protection (DLRP)
- Contact: LCA@conservation.ca.gov
- Website: https://www.conservation.ca.gov/dlrp/lca

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Is Beekeeping Recognized?
**Yes.** Under Government Code §51201:
- **"Agricultural commodity"** means "any and all plant and animal products produced in this state for commercial purposes"
- **"Agricultural use"** means "use of land...for the purpose of producing an agricultural commodity for commercial purposes"
- Beekeeping produces honey, beeswax, propolis, royal jelly, pollen — all agricultural commodities
- Pollination services are an integral agricultural support activity

### Key Considerations for Beekeeping:
- The Williamson Act is **contract-based**, not just an assessment program
- The landowner enters a **minimum 10-year rolling contract** with the county/city
- Land must be within an **Agricultural Preserve** established by the county
- Counties set their own **enrollment guidelines, acreage minimums, and compatible uses**
- Some counties explicitly list apiaries as qualifying; others include them under general agriculture

### Challenges for Beekeeping:
- The **100-acre minimum** for Agricultural Preserves (Gov. Code §51230) can be a barrier
  - However, counties can establish preserves of **less than 100 acres** if they find it necessary due to "unique characteristics of agricultural enterprises"
  - Two or more parcels can be combined to meet the minimum
- County-by-county variation is significant — some counties are very bee-friendly, others less so
- Small parcel beekeeping operations may find it difficult to enter the program

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### Agricultural Preserve Minimum:
- Standard: **100 acres** for an Agricultural Preserve (Gov. Code §51230)
- Counties can allow **smaller preserves** if justified
- Parcels can be combined if contiguous or in common ownership

### County-Level Minimums Vary Widely:
- Some counties: **10 acres** minimum for Williamson Act contract
- Others: **20-40 acres** minimum
- A few: **100 acres** or more
- Some counties have specific minimums for different agricultural uses

### Prime Agricultural Land (Gov. Code §51201(c)):
Prime ag land includes land that:
1. Qualifies as Class I or Class II (NRCS)
2. Rates 80-100 on Storie Index
3. Supports livestock at 1+ animal unit per acre
4. Has fruit/nut trees producing $200+/acre
5. Has returned $200+/acre gross value for 3 of past 5 years

### Non-Prime Agricultural Land:
- Generally requires larger minimum parcels
- Counties typically require 40-160 acres for non-prime

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

California does **not** specify a minimum hive count for Williamson Act eligibility. However:
- Beekeeping must be **commercial** in nature
- Most counties look for evidence of bona fide agricultural production
- Practical minimum: **10-20 hives** for credibility as a commercial operation on Williamson Act land
- Larger parcels may need more hives to demonstrate the land is "primarily" in agricultural use
- California registers beekeepers through the County Agricultural Commissioner

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Step 1: Verify Agricultural Preserve Status
- Check if your land is within an existing Agricultural Preserve
- If not, the county must first establish a preserve (requires public hearing and resolution)

### Step 2: Apply for a Williamson Act Contract
- **Application to:** County Board of Supervisors (or City Council for city preserves)
- Each county has its own application form and procedures
- Some counties have an annual enrollment period

### Step 3: Contract Terms
- **Minimum 10-year term** (standard Land Conservation Contract)
- **Minimum 20-year term** for Farmland Security Zone (FSZ) contracts
- Contracts automatically renew annually unless nonrenewal is filed
- At any time during the contract, either party can file a **notice of nonrenewal**
- After nonrenewal, contract continues for remaining term (9 years from nonrenewal date)

### Application Requirements:
1. Completed county application form
2. Legal description and assessor's parcel number
3. Description of agricultural use
4. Income documentation (if required by county)
5. Application fee (varies by county: $0-$500+)
6. May require a management plan

### Timeline:
- Apply during the county's enrollment period
- Public hearing may be required
- If approved, contract is recorded
- Tax benefit begins the following assessment year

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How Williamson Act Valuation Works:
Under Revenue & Taxation Code §423, Williamson Act land is valued using a **capitalization of income** approach:
- Assessor determines the annual net income from the agricultural use
- Divides by a capitalization rate (set by the State Board of Equalization)
- Result is the "restricted value" for tax purposes
- Tax is based on the **lower of** restricted value or Prop 13 factored base year value

### Typical Savings Examples:

| Scenario | Market Value/Acre | Restricted Value/Acre | Tax Rate | Annual Savings/Acre |
|----------|------------------|---------------------|----------|-------------------|
| Bay Area (Santa Clara) | $200,000 | $1,000-3,000 | 1.10% | ~$2,167 |
| SoCal (Riverside) | $80,000 | $500-2,000 | 1.15% | ~$897 |
| Central Valley (Fresno) | $25,000 | $500-1,500 | 1.10% | ~$259 |
| Central Coast (SLO) | $60,000 | $800-2,500 | 1.05% | ~$604 |
| North State (Shasta) | $15,000 | $200-600 | 1.00% | ~$144 |
| Sierra Foothills (El Dorado) | $40,000 | $300-1,000 | 1.05% | ~$410 |

### Farmland Security Zone (FSZ) Enhancement:
- FSZ contracts offer an additional **35% reduction** below the standard Williamson Act value
- 20-year contract term required
- Only available in certain counties

### Historical State Subvention Payments:
- The state historically reimbursed counties for lost tax revenue through "subvention payments"
- These payments were suspended during budget crises and have been inconsistent
- Some counties have been reluctant to accept new contracts due to lost subvention revenue

California has **58 counties**. County Boards of Supervisors and County Assessors administer the Williamson Act.

### Regional Breakdown:
- **Bay Area (9):** Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Napa, San Francisco*, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Solano, Sonoma (*SF doesn't participate)

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### Average Production by Region:
- **Central Valley:** 50-80 lbs/hive/year
  - Major commercial beekeeping region
  - Almond pollination hub (Feb-March)
  - Citrus, clover, alfalfa, cotton flows
- **Bay Area/Central Coast:** 30-50 lbs/hive/year
  - Diverse floral sources, moderate climate
  - Eucalyptus, sage, wildflower flows
- **SoCal:** 40-60 lbs/hive/year
  - Sage, avocado, citrus, buckwheat flows
  - Year-round mild climate
- **North State/Sierra:** 30-50 lbs/hive/year
  - Star thistle, blackberry, wildflower flows
  - Shorter season at elevation
- **State average:** ~45-65 lbs/hive/year

### California Beekeeping Economics:
- **Almond pollination** is the primary income source for many CA beekeepers: $150-200+/hive rental
- Honey retail: $8-18/lb
- Wholesale: $3-6/lb
- California is the **#1 honey-producing state** in the US
- Major varietal: Orange blossom, sage, star thistle, wildflower

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Nonrenewal (Gov. Code §51245):
- Either party can file a **notice of nonrenewal**
- Contract continues for remaining term (typically 9 more years from filing)
- During the nonrenewal period, assessed value gradually increases from restricted to full market value
- **No penalty** for nonrenewal — this is the intended exit mechanism

### Cancellation (Gov. Code §51280-51287):
Williamson Act contracts can be **cancelled** before expiration, but it's difficult:
- Must petition the county Board of Supervisors
- Board must find either:
  - Cancellation is consistent with the purposes of the Act, OR
  - Cancellation is in the public interest
- **Penalty for cancellation:** 12.5% of the full market value of the land (at unrestricted value)
- This is a substantial penalty — designed to discourage speculative cancellation

### Eminent Domain:
- Government entities can acquire Williamson Act land through eminent domain
- Different valuation rules apply
- May trigger partial cancellation of remaining contract land

### Breach of Contract:
- If landowner uses land in a manner inconsistent with the contract
- County can take enforcement action
- May result in back taxes plus penalties

### Prop 13 Interaction:
- When a Williamson Act contract expires or is cancelled, the property is reassessed at current market value
- This can trigger a **massive property tax increase** since the land was potentially transferred at a Williamson Act value far below market

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### California Homeowner's Exemption:
- California provides a **$7,000 homeowner's exemption** from assessed value (CA Constitution Art. XIII §3(k))
- This applies to owner-occupied primary residences
- Saves approximately $70/year (at 1% tax rate)

### Can You Have Both Williamson Act + Homeowner's Exemption?
- **Yes** — They apply to different aspects of the property
- The **homeowner's exemption** applies to the dwelling/improvements
- The **Williamson Act contract** applies to the agricultural land
- The homesite itself is typically **excluded** from the Williamson Act contract area
- Some counties allow the homesite to be within the preserve but not under contract

### Prop 13 Protection:
- The dwelling/homesite retains its Prop 13 assessed value (purchase price + max 2%/year)
- The agricultural land gets Williamson Act restricted value (typically much lower)
- Both protections apply simultaneously

### Practical Impact:
- The homeowner's exemption is modest ($70/year savings)
- The Williamson Act benefit is **far more significant** (potentially thousands per year)
- There's no conflict between the two programs

## 13. ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Williamson Act Enrollment Trends:
- As of 2024, approximately **15.4 million acres** are enrolled in the Williamson Act statewide
- This represents about **half of California's agricultural land**
- Enrollment has been slowly declining in recent years
- Some counties suspended new enrollment when state subvention payments were cut

### Solar + Agriculture (Agrivoltaics):
- Recent legislation has addressed the intersection of solar energy and Williamson Act
- Solar facilities may be considered "compatible use" in some cases
- Counties must evaluate on a case-by-case basis

### Williamson Act Open Office Hours:
- California Department of Conservation hosts monthly virtual sessions
- Second Thursday of each month, 11am-noon
- Technical assistance on Williamson Act questions
- Contact: LCA@conservation.ca.gov

### County Variation:
- Each county establishes its own:
  - Minimum parcel sizes for enrollment
  - Compatible use rules
  - Enforcement procedures
  - Application requirements
- **Always check with the specific county** before assuming statewide rules apply

### Mapping Tool:
- The Department of Conservation provides a [Williamson Act Enrollment Finder](https://maps.conservation.ca.gov/dlrp/WilliamsonAct/) mapping tool
- Shows enrolled parcels, agricultural preserves, and contract status
