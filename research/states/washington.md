# Washington State Agricultural Property Tax Exemption Research
## Beekeeping Qualification Analysis

**Research Date:** February 10, 2026  
**Status:** INCONCLUSIVE - Beekeeping NOT explicitly confirmed

---

## 1. Does Washington have agricultural use valuation for property taxes?

**YES** - Washington State has a "Current Use Assessment" program under **RCW Chapter 84.34** (Open Space, Agricultural, Timberlands—Current Use—Conservation Futures).

**Legal Citation:** RCW 84.34 (enacted 1970)

The program allows property owners to have their open space, farm and agricultural lands, and timber lands valued at current use rather than fair market value for property tax purposes.

---

## 2. Does beekeeping SPECIFICALLY qualify?

**CANNOT CONFIRM** - Beekeeping is NOT explicitly mentioned in RCW 84.34.020's definition of "farm and agricultural land."

### What the law says:

**RCW 84.34.020(2)** defines "Farm and agricultural land" as land:
- "Devoted primarily to the production of **livestock or agricultural commodities for commercial purposes**"

The statute does NOT specifically list beekeeping, apiculture, or honey production.

### Important context:

**2015 Legislative Change (Senate Bill 6057):**  
Effective July 1, 2015, Washington State designated eligible apiarists as **"farmers" for EXCISE TAX purposes** (B&O tax, sales tax exemptions).

**However:** This designation is explicitly for excise taxes, NOT property taxes. The Washington Department of Revenue's Beekeepers Tax Guide does not address property tax current use assessment.

### County discretion:

Based on research, individual **county assessors** approve current use applications and may interpret "agricultural commodities" to include honey/pollination services. However, there is **no statewide confirmation** that beekeeping qualifies.

**Finding:** You would need to contact your specific county assessor to determine if they accept beekeeping as qualifying agricultural use.

---

## 3. What are the minimum acreage requirements?

The requirements vary based on parcel size, with different income thresholds:

### Tier 1: 20+ acres
- No minimum income requirement
- Must be devoted primarily to production of livestock or agricultural commodities for commercial purposes
- Can be multiple contiguous parcels totaling 20+ acres

### Tier 2: 5-19.9 acres
**Option A (Traditional):**
- Must produce $200+ per acre per year gross income for 3 of the past 5 years

**Option B (Standing crops):**
- Standing crops (fruit trees, vineyards) with expectation of harvest within 7 years
- Demonstrable investment of $100+ per acre in current or previous year

**Option C (Short rotation hardwoods):**
- Expectation of harvest within 15 years
- $100+ per acre investment

### Tier 3: Less than 5 acres
- Must produce $1,500+ per year total gross income for 3 of the past 5 years (as of January 1, 1993)

**Source:** RCW 84.34.020(2)

---

## 4. What are minimum hive requirements (if any)?

**NO STATEWIDE MINIMUM SPECIFIED**

Because beekeeping is not explicitly defined as qualifying agricultural use in RCW 84.34, there are no statutory minimum hive requirements.

**Note:** If a county assessor does accept beekeeping, they would likely determine minimum hive counts based on:
- Achieving required gross income thresholds
- Demonstrating "commercial" operation
- Typical industry standards for commercial beekeeping

**Third-party sources** suggest 6+ colonies may be typical for agricultural classification in some states, but Washington has no official standard.

---

## 5. Application deadline?

**December 31st annually**

Applications for current use classification must be filed between **January 1 and December 31** of the year.

**Source:** RCW 84.34.030

---

## 6. Who administers it?

**County Assessors**

Each county's Department of Assessments administers the Farm and Agricultural Land program.

Applications are:
- Filed with the county assessor
- Reviewed and approved/denied by the assessor
- Subject to county-level interpretation of "agricultural commodities"

**Example:** In King County, contact:
- Elenore Bonyeau, Current Use Specialist
- King County Department of Assessments
- Phone: 206-477-1736

## 7. What's the typical tax savings?

**Not specified in statute, but significant**

The land is assessed at "current use value" based on agricultural productivity rather than market value (highest and best use).

**Typical savings:** 50-90% reduction in assessed value for enrolled land, depending on:
- Location (urban vs. rural)
- Market value differential
- Type of agricultural use

For high-value urban/suburban land, savings can be substantial. For example:
- Market value: $500,000
- Agricultural use value: $50,000-$150,000
- Approximate tax savings: Thousands of dollars annually

**Note:** King County's Public Benefit Rating System (PBRS/Open Space program) offers 50-90% reduction in assessed value.

---

## 8. Any bee registration requirements?

**YES** - Separate from property tax

**Washington State requires annual hive registration** with the Washington State Department of Agriculture (WSDA):

**Cost:** $5 per year (regardless of hive count for small operations)

**Purpose:**
- Disease control and monitoring
- Legal protection from liability claims
- Access to state bee research programs
- Required for commercial beekeepers

**Authority:** Washington State Department of Agriculture apiary program

**Source:** Multiple references in research, including Washington State Beekeepers Association documents

## 9. Gotchas and limitations?

### Major Concerns:

1. **NO EXPLICIT CONFIRMATION FOR BEEKEEPING**
   - Unlike many other states, Washington's RCW 84.34 does NOT specifically list beekeeping, bees, honey, or apiary products
   - Counties may interpret "agricultural commodities" differently
   - Risk of denial or future reclassification

2. **Income Documentation Required**
   - Must demonstrate commercial operation with verifiable gross income
   - Need receipts, sales records, 1099s, etc.
   - Hobby operations will NOT qualify

3. **Removal Penalties**
   - If property is removed from current use classification (voluntarily or by county), owner owes:
     - Back taxes (difference between current use rate and market rate)
     - Additional tax penalty
     - Interest charges
   - Penalties apply for up to 7 years of past taxes

4. **County-by-County Variation**
   - Each county assessor interprets the law
   - What's accepted in King County may be denied in Spokane County
   - No statewide consistency guarantee

5. **Conflicting Tax Status**
   - Beekeepers are "farmers" for EXCISE tax (2015 law)
   - But current use property tax uses older statute (1970s) that doesn't mention bees
   - Legislative disconnect between programs

6. **Minimum Acreage May Be Prohibitive**
   - 20 acres for guaranteed qualification (Tier 1)
   - 5+ acres with $200/acre income requirement
   - Less than 5 acres requires $1,500+ annual income
   - Many urban/suburban beekeepers won't meet minimums

7. **Commercial Operation Standard**
   - Must demonstrate "commercial purposes" and "reasonable expectation of profit"
   - Sideline/hobby operations explicitly excluded
   - Need business plan, marketing, sales history

8. **Annual Monitoring**
   - Counties may require annual certification of continued agricultural use
   - Site inspections possible
   - Must maintain qualifying use continuously

### Contracting Restrictions:

Once enrolled, land use is restricted:
- Cannot develop or subdivide without penalty
- Cannot change to non-agricultural use
- Covenant runs with the land (affects future owners)

## Summary & Recommendation

### The Bottom Line:

**Washington State has current use agricultural assessment (RCW 84.34), but beekeeping is NOT explicitly listed as qualifying agricultural use.**

While the 2015 law designated beekeepers as "farmers" for excise tax purposes, this designation has NOT been incorporated into the property tax statute (RCW 84.34.020).

### If you want to pursue this:

**STEP 1:** Contact your county assessor's office BEFORE applying
- Ask explicitly: "Does your county accept beekeeping as qualifying agricultural use under RCW 84.34?"
- Request their specific requirements (hives per acre, income minimums, etc.)
- Get answer in writing

**STEP 2:** If county says YES:
- Gather documentation:
  - Business license
  - 3-5 years of income records
  - WSDA hive registration
  - Photos of operation
  - Business plan
- Apply by December 31st deadline
- Pay application fee (varies by county)

**STEP 3:** If denied or uncertain:
- Consider advocating for legislative clarification
- Contact Washington State Beekeepers Association (WASBA)
- Lobby for amendment to RCW 84.34.020 to explicitly add "bees and apiary products"

### Risk Assessment: MODERATE TO HIGH

- **Pro:** Some counties may interpret "agricultural commodities" broadly
- **Pro:** 2015 law shows legislative intent to treat beekeepers as farmers
- **Con:** No explicit statutory authority
- **Con:** Risk of denial, appeals, or future reclassification
- **Con:** Potential for back taxes + penalties if removed

**Honest Assessment:** Until RCW 84.34.020 is amended to explicitly include beekeeping (like many other states have done), qualification is uncertain and county-dependent.
