# Arkansas Agricultural Use Valuation - History Requirements

## Research Date: February 10, 2026

## SUMMARY: NO HISTORY REQUIREMENT

Arkansas does **NOT** require a history of prior agricultural use to qualify for agricultural use valuation.

## Key Finding

Arkansas uses a **"current use"** valuation system. Land qualifies for agricultural assessment based on its current agricultural use, not on historical use patterns.

## Statutory Basis

- **Arkansas Code § 26-26-407** - Agricultural or Timberland Use Valuation
- **Arkansas Constitution Amendment 59** - Provides the constitutional authority for use-value assessment

## How It Works

1. **Assessment Based on Current Use**: Agricultural land is assessed based on its value for agricultural purposes, not market value
2. **No Waiting Period**: A new farmer or someone converting land to agricultural use can potentially qualify immediately
3. **Classification by County Assessor**: The county assessor determines if land qualifies as agricultural based on its current use

## Arkansas Agricultural Land Categories

Arkansas assesses agricultural land in classes based on soil productivity:
- Irrigated cropland
- Non-irrigated cropland  
- Pasture/timberland
- Other agricultural uses

## Rollback Tax

While there's no history requirement to qualify, there IS a rollback/recapture tax if land is converted from agricultural to non-agricultural use. This ensures the state recovers some of the tax benefits if agricultural status is abandoned.

## Comparison to Other States

Arkansas's lack of a history requirement makes it one of the more accessible states for new agricultural operations, similar to:
- Florida (no history requirement)
- Louisiana (no history requirement)

Unlike states such as:
- Texas (requires 5/7 years agriculture OR 5/10 years wildlife)
- California (varies by county, often requires history)
