# Florida Agricultural Classification - History Requirements

## Quick Answer

**No mandatory waiting period.** Florida does NOT require a specific number of years of prior agricultural use to qualify. You CAN qualify in year 1.

## Statutory Basis

**F.S. 193.461(3)(b)** states that "only lands that are used primarily for bona fide agricultural purposes shall be classified agricultural."

The statute defines "bona fide agricultural purposes" as **"good faith commercial agricultural use of the land."**

## History as a Factor (Not a Requirement)

Section 193.461(3)(b)(1) lists factors that **"may be taken into consideration"** (emphasis on MAY - not SHALL):

- a. The length of time the land has been so used
- b. Whether the use has been continuous
- c. The purchase price paid
- d. Size (but minimum acreage may NOT be required)
- e. Whether an effort has been made to care for the land per accepted commercial practices
- f. Whether under lease, and terms
- g. Other applicable factors

**Key distinction:** These are discretionary factors the property appraiser MAY consider - they are NOT mandatory requirements. "Length of time" is just one of many factors, and no specific duration is mandated.

## Practical Reality

- Property appraisers have discretion in evaluating applications
- New agricultural operations CAN qualify if they demonstrate genuine commercial intent
- Factors like business plans, income projections, proper care of land, and actual agricultural activity matter
- Some counties may scrutinize new operations more carefully, but the statute does not bar first-year applications

## Comparison to Texas

Unlike Texas (which has a 5-year history requirement under Tax Code § 23.51), Florida's approach is based on **current bona fide use** rather than historical use.
