# Pennsylvania Clean and Green (Act 319) for Beekeeping - Complete Research

**Research Date:** February 9, 2026  
**Purpose:** Build Pennsylvania beekeeping agricultural exemption calculator

---

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: Pennsylvania Farmland and Forest Land Assessment Act of 1974 (Act 319)
**Commonly known as:** "Clean and Green"

#### Key Law:
- **Act 319 of 1974** (P.L. 973, No. 319) — The Pennsylvania Farmland and Forest Land Assessment Act
- Codified in 72 P.S. §5490.1 et seq.
- Implementing regulations: 7 Pa. Code Chapter 137b

#### Three Enrollment Categories:
1. **Agricultural Use** — Land used for agriculture, including beekeeping
2. **Agricultural Reserve** — Land set aside for open-space use (non-commercial agricultural)
3. **Forest Reserve** — Land of 10+ acres used for production of timber products

### How It Works:
Clean and Green allows qualifying agricultural land to be assessed at its **use value** rather than market value for property tax purposes. This is authorized by the Pennsylvania Constitution, Article VIII, §2(b)(ii).

---

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Definition of Agricultural Use
The Act defines "agricultural use" broadly to include:
> Land used for the purpose of producing an agricultural commodity, including the commercial growing of food, fiber, specialty crops, nursery products, **livestock production**, and production of poultry and poultry products.

**✅ Beekeeping qualifies as agricultural use in Pennsylvania** — bees are considered livestock, and honey/beeswax/pollination services are agricultural commodities.

### Additional Recognition:
- Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture actively supports beekeeping
- PA is historically a major beekeeping state
- Honey production, beeswax, pollination services, and bee sales all count as agricultural commodities

### Requirements:
1. Land must have been in agricultural use for the **3 years preceding application**
2. Must be devoted to agricultural use (beekeeping)
3. Land must be **10 acres or more**, OR
4. Land of less than 10 acres must have an **anticipated yearly gross income of at least $2,000** from agricultural activities

---

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State Requirements:
- **10 acres or more:** Qualifies if devoted to agricultural use for 3 prior years (no income requirement)
- **Under 10 acres:** Must demonstrate anticipated yearly gross income of **at least $2,000** from agriculture
- For **Agricultural Reserve:** Minimum 10 acres
- For **Forest Reserve:** Minimum 10 acres

### Special Provisions:
- Contiguous parcels under common ownership may be combined to meet acreage requirements
- Parcels separated by a road may still be considered contiguous
- The 10-acre minimum includes all land under agricultural use (buildings, barns, etc.)

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Requirements:
- **No minimum hive count specified in state law**
- Must demonstrate bona fide agricultural use
- For under 10 acres: must show $2,000/year anticipated gross income

### Practical Hive Estimates:
- At 50 lbs/hive × $10/lb = $500/hive/year from honey alone
- **$2,000 ÷ $500 = approximately 4 hives minimum** to meet income requirement
- Include pollination income, nuc/bee sales for additional revenue
- **Recommended: 6-10 hives** to comfortably exceed income threshold
- For 10+ acres, no income requirement, so even a few hives suffice

---

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Filing:
- Application filed with the **County Assessment Office** (County Board of Assessment Appeals)
- Must be filed by **June 1** for the following tax year
- Some counties accept applications year-round but process annually

### Application Requirements:
- Completed Clean and Green application form
- Description of agricultural use
- Proof of 3 years of agricultural use (or anticipated income for small parcels)
- Property description and acreage
- Application fee varies by county (typically $25-$100)

### Approval Process:
1. County assessment office reviews application
2. May conduct site inspection
3. If approved, use value assessment takes effect
4. **Enrollment is ongoing** — once approved, no annual renewal required
5. Landowner must notify county if use changes

### Ongoing Obligations:
- Must maintain agricultural use
- Must notify county within 30 days of change in use
- Subject to periodic verification by county
- Must allow reasonable inspection by county officials

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How It Works:
- Land assessed at **use value** instead of market value
- Use value is determined by the county based on agricultural productivity
- Buildings used for agricultural purposes included in use value assessment
- Farmhouse and non-agricultural structures assessed at market value

### Typical Use Values vs. Market Values:
| Land Type | Use Value/Acre | Market Value/Acre | Savings |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|---------|
| Prime cropland | $500-$1,500 | $5,000-$25,000 | 80-95% |
| Pasture/hay | $200-$800 | $3,000-$15,000 | 85-95% |
| Woodland/forest | $50-$300 | $2,000-$10,000 | 90-97% |
| Rural land (general) | $150-$600 | $3,000-$15,000 | 85-95% |

### Example Savings (15 acres, southeastern PA):
| Factor | Market Value | Use Value |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Land value (15 acres) | $225,000 | $12,000 |
| Millage rate (assume 50 mills) | $11,250/year | $600/year |
| **Annual savings** | — | **$10,650** |

*Savings are especially dramatic in suburban/exurban areas where land values are high*

## 7. COUNTY STRUCTURE

### Overview:
- Pennsylvania has **67 counties**
- Property tax is administered at the county level
- The **County Board of Assessment Appeals** (or Assessment Office) handles Clean and Green enrollment
- Philadelphia has a separate system

### Key Offices:
- **County Assessment Office / Board of Assessment Appeals** — Handles property assessment, Clean and Green applications
- **County Treasurer** — Collects property taxes
- Tax rates set by **county, municipality, and school district** (three separate tax bills in most areas)

### Assessment Practices:
- Pennsylvania has no statewide reassessment requirement
- Some counties haven't reassessed in decades (leading to very outdated base-year values)
- **Common Level Ratio (CLR)** adjusts between assessed value and market value
- Clean and Green overrides this with use value assessment

## 8. AVERAGE TAX RATES

### State Overview:
- Pennsylvania has **moderately high property taxes**
- **State average effective rate:** approximately 1.53%
- Rates vary dramatically — some of the highest in the nation in certain school districts

### Tax Rate Structure:
- Expressed in **mills** (millage rate)
- Three separate taxing bodies: county, municipality, school district
- School district taxes often account for 60-70% of total
- No state property tax

### Notable County Rates (approximate total millage / effective rates):
- **Chester County (SE PA):** ~45-65 mills, effective 1.4%
- **Montgomery County (suburbs of Philly):** ~40-60 mills, effective 1.3%
- **Bucks County:** ~45-60 mills, effective 1.2%
- **Lancaster County:** ~40-55 mills, effective 1.5%
- **Allegheny County (Pittsburgh):** ~50-70 mills, effective 1.8%
- **Philadelphia:** ~58 mills, effective 1.4%
- **Centre County (State College):** ~45-55 mills, effective 1.3%
- **Rural PA counties:** ~30-50 mills, effective 0.8-1.2%

*Note: Due to outdated assessments in many counties, stated millage rates don't directly translate to effective rates*

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### Pennsylvania Climate & Beekeeping:
- **Climate zone:** Humid continental (USDA zones 5b-7a)
- **Nectar flow:** Primarily May through August
- **Major nectar sources:** Clover, goldenrod, black locust, basswood (linden), tulip poplar, asters, fruit trees (apple, cherry), buckwheat, wildflowers

### Average Production:
- **State average yield:** 45-60 lbs/hive/year
- **Southeast PA:** 50-65 lbs/hive (warmer, diverse agricultural area)
- **South Central PA (Lancaster/York):** 55-70 lbs/hive (excellent agricultural base)
- **Northeast PA:** 40-55 lbs/hive (higher elevation, shorter season)
- **Northwest PA:** 40-50 lbs/hive (lake effect, shorter season)
- **Central PA:** 45-60 lbs/hive (mountain valleys, varied)
- **Southwest PA (Pittsburgh area):** 50-60 lbs/hive

### Revenue Potential:
- Local honey: $10-$18/lb retail (higher in suburban Philadelphia/Pittsburgh)
- Wholesale: $5-$8/lb
- Pollination services: $75-$150/colony for fruit orchards
- 10 hives × 55 lbs × $12/lb = **$6,600/year potential gross**

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Rollback Taxes (Act 319, Section 5.1):
When land enrolled in Clean and Green is converted to non-qualifying use:

#### Roll-Back Tax:
- **7 years** of roll-back taxes
- Calculated as: difference between taxes paid under use value and taxes that would have been paid at market value
- Plus **6% interest per year** on the deferred taxes
- Roll-back tax is a lien on the property

### What Triggers Roll-Back:
1. **Change to non-qualifying use** (development, etc.)
2. **Splitting/separation of land** from enrolled tract (with exceptions)
3. **Breach of conditions** of enrollment

### Exceptions to Roll-Back:
- Transfer to immediate family member (if use continues)
- Land taken by eminent domain
- Construction of residence for owner or family member (if less than 2 acres)
- Agricultural-related structures
- Small non-agricultural commercial enterprise (less than 2 acres, if authorized by county)
- Oil/gas/mineral extraction (with restrictions)

### Impact:
- Roll-back can be **very expensive** — 7 years of deferred taxes plus interest
- In areas with high market values, this can amount to tens of thousands of dollars
- This serves as a significant deterrent to voluntary conversion

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Can You Have Both? **YES** (with caveats)

#### Pennsylvania Homestead Exclusion:
- Available through the **Taxpayer Relief Act (Act 1 of 2006)**
- Provides property tax relief funded by casino revenue
- Amount varies by school district (typically $500-$2,000/year)
- No income or age requirement (all primary residences qualify)

#### PA Property Tax/Rent Rebate Program:
- For seniors 65+, widows/widowers 50+, disabled 18+
- Income limit: $45,000 for homeowners, $45,000 for renters
- Rebate up to $1,000 on property taxes or rent

#### Interaction with Clean and Green:
- **Clean and Green and homestead/rebate programs can be combined**
- Clean and Green reduces assessment on agricultural land
- Homestead exclusion reduces school tax on the dwelling
- Property tax rebate provides additional relief for qualifying seniors
- The farmhouse/dwelling is assessed at market value (separate from agricultural land)

#### Practical Application:
1. Agricultural land = use value assessment (Clean and Green)
2. Farmhouse/dwelling = market value with homestead exclusion (school tax reduction)
3. Eligible seniors also get property tax rebate
4. All three benefits can apply simultaneously

## ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Pennsylvania-Specific Considerations:
1. **Clean and Green is extremely popular** — hundreds of thousands of acres enrolled across PA
2. **7-year roll-back** is one of the longest in the nation — significant deterrent
3. **County assessment practices vary wildly** — some counties haven't reassessed since the 1970s
4. **Three separate tax bills** (county, municipal, school) mean savings affect all three
5. **Lancaster County** has some of the highest agricultural productivity in the eastern US
6. **PA has a strong Amish/Mennonite farming community** that heavily uses Clean and Green
7. **Coal/gas/mineral rights** interaction with Clean and Green can be complex in western PA

### PA Beekeeping Registration:
- Pennsylvania requires registration of apiaries with the PA Department of Agriculture
- **PA Apiary Inspection Program** — mandatory annual inspection
- Registration fee: nominal
- Contact: PA Dept of Agriculture, Bureau of Plant Industry, (717) 772-5225
- Registration supports Clean and Green applications
