# Virginia Land Use Assessment for Beekeeping - Complete Research

**Research Date:** February 9, 2026  
**Purpose:** Build Virginia beekeeping agricultural exemption calculator

---

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statutes: Code of Virginia §58.1-3229 through §58.1-3244
**Also known as:** "Land Use Assessment" or "Use Value Assessment"

#### Key Sections:
- **§58.1-3230** — Defines special classifications: agricultural, horticultural, forest, and open-space use
- **§58.1-3231** — Authority for localities to adopt land-use assessment ordinances
- **§58.1-3233** — Determinations required before assessment (including minimum acreage)
- **§58.1-3234** — Application process
- **§58.1-3236** — Valuation methodology
- **§58.1-3237** — Roll-back taxes on change of use

#### Constitutional Basis:
- **Virginia Constitution, Article X, §2** — Authorizes the General Assembly to define and classify real estate devoted to agricultural, horticultural, forest, or open space uses for special assessment

### How It Works:
- Virginia **does not mandate** land use assessment statewide — each locality must **adopt an ordinance** to implement it
- Virtually all rural Virginia counties have adopted such ordinances
- Some cities and towns have also adopted them
- The **State Land Evaluation Advisory Council (SLEAC)** publishes recommended use values annually

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Definition of Agricultural Use (§58.1-3230):
> "Real estate devoted to agricultural use" shall mean real estate devoted to the **bona fide production for sale of plants and animals**, or products made from such plants and animals on the real estate.

**✅ Beekeeping qualifies as agricultural use in Virginia** — bees are animals, honey/beeswax/pollination services are products from animals, and production for sale qualifies.

### Key Considerations:
- Must be "bona fide production **for sale**" — cannot be purely hobbyist
- Must be **commercial in nature** — intent to sell products
- Beekeeping qualifies as producing "animals" and "products made from such animals"
- The land itself must be devoted to the agricultural use

### Uniform Standards:
- The **Commissioner of Agriculture and Consumer Services** prescribes uniform standards
- Local assessors apply these standards in determining qualification
- Prior use of the property for a minimum length of time may be required (typically 5 years, but localities may waive for certain long-maturity crops)

---

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State Requirements (§58.1-3233):
- **Agricultural/Horticultural use:** Minimum **5 acres**
- **Forest use:** Minimum **20 acres**
- **Open-space use:** Minimum **5 acres** (localities may set higher)

### Special Provisions:
- **Aquaculture and specialty crops:** Local governing body may by ordinance prescribe **less than 5 acres**
- Contiguous parcels under common ownership may be combined
- Properties separated by a public right-of-way are considered contiguous
- Agricultural/forestal district members: non-contiguous parcels may be deemed contiguous

### For Beekeeping:
- The **5-acre minimum** applies to beekeeping as agricultural use
- Some localities may allow less for specialty agricultural activities
- Beekeeping on less than 5 acres may qualify if the locality has adopted a reduced-acreage ordinance

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Requirements:
- **No minimum hive count specified in state law**
- Must demonstrate "bona fide production for sale"
- Locality assessors evaluate on case-by-case basis

### Practical Guidance:
- Most assessors expect evidence of commercial agricultural activity
- **Recommended: 6-12 hives** for most Virginia localities
- Sales records, farm plan, and registration with VDACS (Virginia Dept of Agriculture and Consumer Services) strengthen applications
- Pollination contracts count as commercial agricultural activity

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Filing:
- Application filed with the **Commissioner of Revenue** or **local assessing officer**
- Initial application must be filed **at least 60 days** before the tax year

### Application Timeline:
- During **general reassessment years:** Application may be filed until 30 days after assessment increase notice is mailed (or 60 days before tax year, whichever is later)
- Localities with fiscal tax years: 60 days before effective date of assessment

### Late Filing:
- Local governing body **may** permit late filing within **60 days** after deadline
- Late filing requires payment of a late filing fee (set by locality)
- Additional extension possible up to 30 days after assessment notices mailed

### Revalidation:
- Localities may require **revalidation every 6 years** (not annual renewal)
- Revalidation is a simplified form confirming continued qualifying use
- Revalidation fee may be charged (cannot exceed initial application fee)

### Application Requirements:
- Completed application form (prescribed by Virginia Department of Taxation)
- Description of property and agricultural use
- Evidence of bona fide agricultural activity
- Application fee (set by locality, typically $25-$100)

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How It Works:
- Land assessed at **use value** instead of fair market value
- **SLEAC (State Land Evaluation Advisory Council)** publishes recommended use values annually
- Localities may adopt SLEAC values or establish their own
- Farmhouse and non-agricultural structures assessed at market value

## 7. COUNTY STRUCTURE

### Overview:
- Virginia has **95 counties** and **38 independent cities**
- Independent cities are NOT part of any county
- Property tax is administered by the **Commissioner of Revenue** (or City Assessor in cities)

### Key Offices:
- **Commissioner of Revenue** — Handles property assessment, land use applications
- **County/City Treasurer** — Collects property taxes
- **Board of Equalization** — Hears assessment appeals
- **Local governing body (Board of Supervisors)** — Adopts land use ordinance, sets tax rates

### Assessment Practices:
- Virginia requires reassessment at least every **4 years** (varies by locality; some do annually)
- Tax rates expressed as **dollars per $100 of assessed value**
- Land use assessment must be adopted by local ordinance (§58.1-3231)
- Nearly all rural counties have adopted land use ordinances

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### Virginia Climate & Beekeeping:
- **Climate zone:** Humid subtropical to humid continental (USDA zones 6a-8a)
- **Nectar flow:** Primarily April through September
- **Major nectar sources:** Tulip poplar, black locust, clover, sourwood, goldenrod, asters, autumn olive, fruit trees, wildflowers

### Average Production:
- **State average yield:** 45-65 lbs/hive/year
- **Northern Virginia:** 40-55 lbs/hive (suburban encroachment, but good nectar sources)
- **Shenandoah Valley:** 50-65 lbs/hive (excellent agricultural area)
- **Piedmont:** 50-60 lbs/hive (diverse flora)
- **Tidewater:** 50-65 lbs/hive (longer season, coastal flora)
- **Southwest VA:** 50-70 lbs/hive (excellent sourwood, mountain flora)
- **Southside VA:** 50-65 lbs/hive (tobacco country transitioning, good wildflowers)

### Revenue Potential:
- Local honey: $10-$18/lb retail (higher in NoVA/urban areas)
- Wholesale: $5-$8/lb
- Pollination services: $75-$150/colony
- 10 hives × 55 lbs × $12/lb = **$6,600/year potential gross**

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Roll-Back Taxes (§58.1-3237):
When qualifying use changes to non-qualifying use:

#### Standard Roll-Back (non-sliding scale localities):
- **5 years** of deferred tax recouped
- Deferred tax = difference between tax paid under use value and tax that would have been paid at fair market value
- Plus **simple interest** at a rate set by governing body (not exceeding delinquent tax rate)
- Current year extended at fair market value

#### Sliding Scale Roll-Back:
- If locality adopted a sliding scale ordinance: roll-back covers entire period from effective date of agreement
- Plus simple interest at governing body-set rate

### What Triggers Roll-Back:
1. **Change in use** to non-qualifying use
2. **Rezoning to more intensive use** at owner's request
3. Owner must report change within **60 days** to Commissioner of Revenue

### What Does NOT Trigger Roll-Back:
- Change of ownership (if new owner continues qualifying use and doesn't rezone)
- Rezoning NOT requested by owner

### Key Details:
- Roll-back taxes are assessed against the owner at time of conversion
- Must be paid within 30 days of assessment
- If over $10 threshold, lien attaches to property
- Owner has 60 days to report change to Commissioner of Revenue

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Can You Have Both? **YES**

#### Virginia Tax Relief Programs:
1. **Real Estate Tax Relief for Elderly/Disabled (§58.1-3210 et seq.)**
   - Available to persons 65+ or permanently disabled
   - Income and net worth limits set by each locality
   - Can provide full or partial exemption from real estate tax
   
2. **Tax Deferral Program**
   - Available in some localities for elderly/disabled
   - Taxes deferred until property sold or transferred

#### Interaction with Land Use Assessment:
- **Land use assessment and tax relief programs can be combined**
- Land use assessment reduces the value of agricultural land
- Tax relief programs can further reduce or exempt tax on the dwelling
- Both apply to the same property simultaneously

#### Practical Application:
1. Agricultural land (beekeeping) = use value assessment
2. Dwelling = market value with potential tax relief (if qualifying)
3. Both benefits can apply simultaneously
4. In Northern Virginia, this combination can save property owners many thousands annually

## ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Virginia-Specific Considerations:
1. **Land use assessment is locally adopted** — each county must have an ordinance; virtually all rural counties do
2. **SLEAC recommended values** guide localities but aren't mandatory
3. **Northern Virginia savings are extraordinary** — land worth $50,000-$100,000/acre can be assessed at $50-$500/acre
4. **Independent cities** have separate assessment systems from counties
5. **Agricultural/Forestal Districts** (§15.2-4300 et seq.) provide additional protections and can help smaller parcels qualify
6. **Virginia is a major beekeeping state** — Virginia State Beekeepers Association is very active
7. **5-acre minimum** is lower than many states, making Virginia accessible for beekeeping qualification
8. **Minimum use period** (typically 5 years for new land, but can be reduced for certain uses) — check with local Commissioner of Revenue

### Virginia Apiary Registration:
- Virginia requires registration of apiaries with VDACS (Virginia Dept of Agriculture and Consumer Services)
- **State Apiarist** oversees bee health and inspection
- Contact: VDACS Office of Plant Industry Services, (804) 786-3515
- Registration strengthens land use assessment applications

### Agricultural/Forestal Districts:
- Voluntary program under §15.2-4300 et seq.
- Provides additional protections against government-initiated rezoning and public utility expansion
- Can help non-contiguous parcels qualify by deeming them contiguous
- Districts are created at the local level with state oversight
- **Recommended for beekeepers** in areas facing development pressure

### Beekeeping Resources:
- Virginia State Beekeepers Association (VSBA)
- Virginia Tech Extension — Apiculture Program
- Virginia Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (VDACS) — Apiary Program
- Commissioner of Revenue offices for land use assessment applications
- State Land Evaluation Advisory Council (SLEAC) — publishes use values
