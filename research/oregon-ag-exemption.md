**Research Date:** February 9, 2026

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: ORS 308A — Land Special Assessments (Farm Use Special Assessment)
**Source:** [ORS Chapter 308A](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors308a.html)

Oregon's agricultural property tax program is the **"Farm Use Special Assessment"** system. It has two distinct tracks based on zoning:

1. **Exclusive Farm Use (EFU) Zone** — Land within EFU-zoned areas automatically qualifies (ORS 308A.062)
2. **Non-Exclusive Farm Use Zone** — Land outside EFU zones must meet income requirements (ORS 308A.068)

### Key Code Sections:
- **ORS 308A.050** — Legislative intent
- **ORS 308A.053** — Definitions
- **ORS 308A.056** — Definition of "farm use" (**explicitly includes honeybees**)
- **ORS 308A.062** — Qualification in EFU zones
- **ORS 308A.068** — Qualification in non-EFU zones
- **ORS 308A.071** — Income requirements for non-EFU farmland
- **ORS 308A.092** — Determining value for farm use
- **ORS 308A.113** — Disqualification from EFU farmland
- **ORS 308A.116** — Disqualification from non-EFU farmland

### Related Law:
- **ORS 215.203-215.311** — Exclusive Farm Use zoning provisions
- **ORS 215.283** — Uses permitted in EFU zones
- **OAR 150-308A** — Oregon Department of Revenue administrative rules

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Is Beekeeping Explicitly Recognized?
**YES — Explicitly listed in statute.**

ORS 308A.056(1)(b) defines "farm use" as including:
> *"Feeding, breeding, managing or selling livestock, poultry, fur-bearing animals or **honeybees** or the produce thereof."*

This is one of the clearest statutory recognitions of beekeeping as a qualifying agricultural use in any state.

### Key Rules:
- Beekeeping is a **primary qualifying farm use**, not a secondary or alternative use
- The land must be employed for the **primary purpose of obtaining a profit in money**
- Both honey production and pollination services qualify
- Selling bees, queens, nucleus colonies, beeswax, and other hive products all qualify

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE & INCOME REQUIREMENTS

### Two-Track System Based on Zoning:

#### Track 1: Exclusive Farm Use (EFU) Zone (ORS 308A.062)
- **No minimum acreage requirement**
- **No minimum income requirement**
- Land in an EFU zone that is used for farm use automatically qualifies
- This is the easiest path — if your land is zoned EFU, just use it for beekeeping
- Most rural Oregon land is zoned EFU

#### Track 2: Non-Exclusive Farm Use Zone (ORS 308A.068 & 308A.071)
- Income requirements apply based on soil class and acreage:

| Soil Class | Acreage | Annual Gross Income Required |
|-----------|---------|----------------------------|
| Class I-IV (higher capability) | Any | $650/year from farm use |
| Class V-VIII (lower capability) | Less than 30 acres | Not eligible unless income met |
| Class V-VIII | 30+ acres | $650/year from farm use |
| Any class | Less than 6.5 acres with higher capability OR less than 15 acres without | Higher scrutiny |

- Income must be from **actual farm use** on the land
- Must show income for the **preceding tax year** or **3 of the preceding 5 years**
- First-year applicants may qualify based on reasonable expectation of income

### Wasteland Provisions (ORS 308A.074):
- Wasteland (not economically tillable or grazeable) within EFU zones qualifies
- Adjacent to and in common ownership with farm use land
- Must apply annually

### No State-Mandated Minimum Hive Count:
- Oregon does not specify minimum hives
- Practical minimum: 3-6 hives to generate qualifying income
- Oregon beekeepers must register with Oregon Dept of Agriculture under ORS 602

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

Oregon has **no statutory minimum hive requirement**. However:
- Income thresholds of $650/year for non-EFU zone land require at least a few productive hives
- In EFU zones, even a single hive could technically qualify (though assessors look for bona fide commercial intent)
- Recommended minimum for credibility: **4-6 hives**
- Average honey production in Oregon: **35-55 lbs/hive** (varies by region)

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### For EFU Zone Land:
- **Automatic qualification** — County assessor identifies EFU-zoned land and applies farm use valuation
- No application typically required for land already zoned EFU and used for farming
- Assessor may request verification of farm use

### For Non-EFU Zone Land:
- **Application required** — File with the county assessor's office
- Use Form **150-310-075** (Application for Farm Use Special Assessment)
- Must include income documentation
- Application filed for the year in which assessment is sought

### Deadlines:
- Non-EFU applications: Generally by **April 1** for the current tax year (or by assessor's filing deadline)
- Some counties may accept late applications with penalties

### What to Include:
1. Completed application form
2. Farm income records (Schedule F, sales receipts)
3. Evidence of farm use activity (beekeeping operation photos, hive registration)
4. Property description and parcel numbers
5. Soil class information (often available from county)

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How Farm Use Special Assessment Works:
- Land is valued based on its **income-producing capability for farm use** rather than market value
- Oregon uses an **income approach** to determine farm use value (ORS 308A.092)
- The farm use value is typically **far below market value**, especially near urban areas

### Typical Savings Examples:

| Scenario | Market Value/Acre | Farm Use Value/Acre | Tax Rate | Annual Savings/Acre |
|----------|------------------|-------------------|----------|-------------------|
| Portland metro (Clackamas) | $100,000 | $500-1,500 | 1.20% | ~$1,182 |
| Willamette Valley (Marion) | $40,000 | $600-2,000 | 1.25% | ~$475 |
| Central Oregon (Deschutes) | $50,000 | $200-500 | 1.15% | ~$569 |
| Eastern Oregon (Umatilla) | $8,000 | $150-400 | 1.30% | ~$99 |
| Coast (Lincoln) | $30,000 | $200-600 | 1.00% | ~$294 |

### Farm Use Values:
- Set by the county assessor using DOR guidelines
- Based on soil capability, irrigation, and agricultural income potential
- Range: **$50/acre** (dryland range) to **$3,000/acre** (irrigated prime cropland in Willamette Valley)

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### Average Production by Region:
- **Willamette Valley:** 40-60 lbs/hive/year
  - Excellent forage: clover, blackberry, wildflowers
  - Mild, rainy climate extends blooming season
- **Portland Metro:** 35-50 lbs/hive/year
  - Urban/suburban forage diversity
  - Shorter flights to diverse nectar sources
- **Coast:** 25-40 lbs/hive/year
  - Cooler, wetter climate limits production
  - Fireweed, salal, blackberry flows
- **Central Oregon:** 30-50 lbs/hive/year
  - Drier, hot summers, cold winters
  - Alfalfa, clover, wildflower flows
- **Eastern Oregon:** 40-70 lbs/hive/year
  - Irrigated agriculture provides excellent forage
  - Alfalfa, clover, star thistle
- **State average:** ~40-55 lbs/hive/year

### Honey Prices (Oregon):
- Retail: $9-16/lb for local/artisanal
- Wholesale: $4-7/lb
- Oregon-specific varietals (meadowfoam, fireweed): $15-25/lb premium

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Disqualification from Farm Use Special Assessment:

#### EFU Zone Land (ORS 308A.113):
- Disqualification occurs when land is **no longer being used for farm use**
- Assessor determines land no longer qualifies and sends notice
- **Potential additional taxes** apply

#### Non-EFU Zone Land (ORS 308A.116):
- Disqualification for failing to meet income requirements
- Disqualification for change of use
- Additional taxes are imposed

### Additional Tax Calculation (ORS 308A.703):
- Additional taxes equal the **difference between what was paid under farm use assessment and what would have been paid at real market value**
- Calculated for **up to 10 years** of back taxes (depending on circumstances)
- Interest accrues on the additional tax amounts

### Exceptions (ORS 308A.709):
No additional taxes if disqualification due to:
- Government acquisition for public use
- Natural disaster making farm use impracticable
- Certain zone changes by the government
- Land subdivided consistent with applicable law

### Remediation Plans (ORS 308A.091):
- If farm use is temporarily impossible due to severe adverse conditions
- Owner can file a **remediation plan** certified by OSU Extension
- Prevents disqualification during remediation period

### Change of Assessment (ORS 308A.724):
- Disqualified land can apply for different special assessment (e.g., open space)
- Must meet requirements of the new classification

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Oregon Does NOT Have a General Homestead Exemption
Oregon does not provide a standard homestead property tax exemption.

### Farm and Forest Homesites (ORS 308A.250-259):
- Oregon provides a **special homesite assessment** for farm/forest properties
- The homesite portion of farm or forest land is assessed at a **special value**
- The homesite is the dwelling and surrounding land used in conjunction with the dwelling

### Can You Have Both?
- **Farm Use Special Assessment** applies to the agricultural land
- **Farm Homesite Assessment** can apply to the dwelling/homesite area
- These work **together** — you don't have to choose one or the other
- The homesite gets a special (lower) value, and the farmland gets farm use value

### Other Oregon Property Tax Programs:
- **Senior/Disabled Property Tax Deferral** (ORS 311.666) — Can be combined with farm use
- **Oregon Property Tax Exemption for Veterans** — Separate from farm use
- **Measure 50 limits** — Assessed value cannot increase more than 3% per year

## 13. ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Oregon's Unique EFU Zoning System:
- Oregon's statewide land use planning (Goal 3) requires counties to zone farmland as EFU
- Over **16 million acres** of Oregon farmland are in EFU zones
- EFU zoning provides **both** land use protection AND tax benefits
- Development on EFU land is severely restricted
- This makes Oregon somewhat unique — the zoning itself protects agricultural land value

### Non-EFU Farm Use — Urban Fringe Benefits:
- Even small parcels in non-EFU zones near Portland, Salem, Eugene can qualify
- The income threshold of $650/year is very achievable with a modest beekeeping operation
- Tax savings near urban areas can be enormous (market values high, farm use values low)

### Measure 50 Considerations:
- Oregon's Measure 50 (1997) froze assessed values at 1995-96 levels, growing max 3%/year
- **Farm use value** overrides the Measure 50 value when lower
- When farm use value exceeds the Measure 50 value, Measure 50 value applies
- This creates additional complexity in calculating exact tax savings
