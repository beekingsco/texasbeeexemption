# Washington State Agricultural Tax Exemption for Beekeeping — Complete Research

**Research Date:** February 9, 2026  
**Purpose:** Build Washington beekeeping agricultural classification calculator

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: RCW 84.34 — Open Space, Agricultural, Timberlands — Current Use — Conservation Futures
**Source:** [RCW Chapter 84.34](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=84.34)

Washington's agricultural property tax program is called the **"Current Use" program**. It allows qualifying agricultural land to be assessed at its current use value rather than its highest and best use (market) value.

### Key Code Sections:
- **RCW 84.34.010** — Legislative declaration
- **RCW 84.34.020** — Definitions (farm and agricultural land)
- **RCW 84.34.030** — Applications for current use classification
- **RCW 84.34.035** — Approval or denial of applications
- **RCW 84.34.060** — Determination of true and fair value of classified land
- **RCW 84.34.065** — Determination of true and fair value of farm and agricultural land
- **RCW 84.34.108** — Removal of classification (rollback penalties)

### Related Code:
- **WAC 458-30** — Department of Revenue administrative rules for current use
- **RCW 84.34.020(2)** — Defines "Farm and agricultural land" in three tiers based on acreage

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Is Beekeeping Explicitly Recognized?
**Yes.** Under RCW 84.34.020(2), "farm and agricultural land" is land "devoted primarily to the production of livestock or **agricultural commodities** for commercial purposes." Beekeeping (apiculture) is an agricultural commodity production activity. Honey, beeswax, and pollination services are recognized agricultural products.

Washington State Department of Agriculture registers beekeepers under **RCW 15.60** (Apiaries), which further establishes beekeeping as a recognized agricultural activity.

### Key Rules for Beekeeping Qualification:
- Land must be devoted **primarily** to agricultural production
- Must be for **commercial purposes** (not hobby)
- Income thresholds must be met for smaller parcels
- The beekeeping operation should demonstrate commercial intent through sales records, registered hive inspections, etc.

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE & INCOME REQUIREMENTS

### Three-Tier System (RCW 84.34.020(2)):

#### Tier 1: 20+ Acres (RCW 84.34.020(2)(a))
- **20 or more acres** (single parcel or contiguous parcels)
- Devoted primarily to production of livestock or agricultural commodities for commercial purposes
- **No income requirement** — just needs to be devoted to agriculture
- Easiest path for beekeepers with larger properties

#### Tier 2: 5–19.99 Acres (RCW 84.34.020(2)(b))
- Must produce **$200 or more per acre per year** gross income
- For **three of the five calendar years** preceding application
- Example: 10-acre parcel needs $2,000/year in agricultural income
- Beekeeping income from honey, wax, pollination services all count

#### Tier 3: Under 5 Acres (RCW 84.34.020(2)(c))
- Must produce **$1,500 or more per year** total gross income
- For **three of the five calendar years** preceding application
- This applies regardless of specific acreage (1-4.99 acres)

#### Alternative for 5-19.99 Acres (RCW 84.34.020(2)(d)):
- Standing crops with expectation of harvest within 7 years
- AND demonstrable investment of $100+ per acre
- Less applicable to beekeeping since hives are not "standing crops"

### State-Level Minimums:
- **No state-mandated minimum acreage** — even parcels under 1 acre can qualify if income thresholds are met
- **No state-mandated minimum hive count** — determined by income requirements
- Practical minimum: ~4-6 hives to meet income thresholds on small parcels
- County assessors may apply additional scrutiny to very small parcels

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

Washington does **not** specify a minimum number of hives in statute. However:
- Hive registration is required under RCW 15.60 with the WA Dept of Agriculture
- To meet income thresholds on smaller parcels, typically need 4-10 hives minimum
- Average honey production in WA: **40-60 lbs per hive** (western WA tends lower due to rain; eastern WA higher)
- At ~$8-12/lb retail for local honey, each hive can produce $320-720/year
- Pollination services can add significant additional income

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Where to Apply:
- **County Assessor's Office** — Applications for farm and agricultural land (RCW 84.34.020(2)) are made to the county assessor
- Forms are prepared by the WA Department of Revenue and supplied by county assessors

### When to Apply:
- **Applications must be made during the calendar year preceding the year classification begins** (RCW 84.34.030(2))
- Example: Apply in 2026 for classification beginning January 1, 2027
- No specific deadline within the year, but earlier is better

### Application Requirements:
1. Complete application form (WA DOR form)
2. Reasonable processing fee (set by county — typically $25-$100)
3. Notarized verification of truth
4. Statement acknowledging potential tax liability upon removal
5. Income documentation (for parcels under 20 acres)
6. Evidence of commercial agricultural activity

### Approval Process:
- County assessor reviews application
- May request additional information
- Assessor approves or denies
- If approved, classification begins January 1 of the following year
- Owner receives notice of approval/disapproval (RCW 84.34.050)
- Classification continues automatically until removal

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How It Works:
- Land is assessed at **current use value** (agricultural productivity value) rather than market value
- The difference between market value and current use value represents potential tax savings
- Savings are most dramatic in areas with high land values near urban centers

### Typical Savings Examples:

| Scenario | Market Value/Acre | Ag Value/Acre | Tax Rate | Annual Savings/Acre |
|----------|------------------|---------------|----------|-------------------|
| King County suburban | $150,000 | $300-800 | 1.00% | ~$1,492 |
| Pierce County rural | $50,000 | $200-600 | 1.10% | ~$543 |
| Yakima County ag | $15,000 | $300-1,000 | 1.20% | ~$168 |
| Spokane suburban | $30,000 | $200-500 | 1.10% | ~$325 |

### Agricultural Productivity Values:
- Vary by county and soil classification
- Range from **$50/acre** (dryland/range) to **$2,000/acre** (irrigated prime cropland)
- Beekeeping land typically assessed at $150-500/acre depending on county
- County assessors use DOR guidelines for productivity values

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### Average Production by Region:
- **Western WA (west of Cascades):** 30-50 lbs/hive/year
  - Higher rainfall, moderate temps, shorter season
  - Main flows: blackberry, maple, clover, fireweed
- **Eastern WA (east of Cascades):** 50-80 lbs/hive/year
  - Drier climate, longer hot summers
  - Main flows: alfalfa, clover, wildflowers, fruit tree pollination
- **State average:** ~45-60 lbs/hive/year

### Honey Prices (WA):
- Retail: $8-15/lb for local/artisanal honey
- Wholesale: $4-7/lb
- Varietal premium honeys (fireweed, buckwheat): up to $18-25/lb

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY (RCW 84.34.108)

### When Land is Removed from Classification:
Removal triggers **additional tax, interest, and penalty** calculated as:

#### Additional Tax (as of September 1, 2025):
- For **farm and agricultural land**: Difference between taxes paid at current use and what would have been paid at market value for the **4 years** last past (reduced from 7 years by 2024 legislation)
- For **other classified land (open space, timber)**: 7 years of back taxes

#### Interest:
- Calculated at the statutory delinquent property tax rate
- Applied from the dates additional tax could have been paid

#### Penalty:
- As provided in RCW 84.34.080
- May be waived if removal meets certain conditions (owner-requested withdrawal vs. involuntary removal)

### Exceptions to Rollback (RCW 84.34.108(6)):
No additional tax if removal due to:
- Transfer to government in exchange for other WA land
- Eminent domain taking
- Natural disaster (flood, wildfire, earthquake, windstorm)
- Government action disallowing present use
- Transfer to a church (tax-exempt)
- State agency acquisition for conservation

### Rollback Becomes a Lien:
- Attaches when land is removed from classification
- Priority over all other liens
- Can be foreclosed like delinquent property taxes

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Washington State Does NOT Have a Traditional Homestead Exemption
- Washington has **no general homestead property tax exemption** for all homeowners
- Instead, Washington has limited programs:
  - **Senior/Disabled Exemption** (RCW 84.36.381) — for seniors 61+ or disabled with income limits
  - **Property Tax Deferral Program** — defer a portion of taxes

### Can You Have Both?
- **Current Use (Ag) Classification** can be combined with **Senior/Disabled Exemption** if eligible
- The farm operator's residence can be included in the current use classification under RCW 84.34.020(2)(f) if:
  - The residence is on or contiguous to the classified parcel
  - The use of the residence is integral to agricultural purposes
- The residence site itself is typically **not** given ag valuation — only the agricultural land

### Practical Impact:
- Most WA property owners benefit most from the Current Use agricultural classification
- No "stacking" conflict since WA doesn't have a standard homestead exemption
- Senior/disabled exemption provides additional relief if owner qualifies

## 13. ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Agrivoltaic Facilities:
- As of recent legislation, land with **agrivoltaic facilities** (solar + agriculture) can qualify for farm and agricultural land classification (RCW 84.34.020(2)(i))

### Incidental Use:
- Up to **20% of classified farm land** can be used for incidental compatible uses
- Includes: wetland preservation, produce stands, farm festivals, weddings (with existing appurtenances)
- This can be relevant for agritourism combined with beekeeping

### Cannabis:
- Cannabis operations have specific provisions under RCW 84.34.410
- Cannabis land does NOT automatically qualify for current use

### Transfer Provisions:
- New buyers must sign a **Notice of Classification Continuance** at time of sale
- If not signed, all rollback taxes become due immediately from the seller
- County auditor will not record the conveyance without continuance or payment
