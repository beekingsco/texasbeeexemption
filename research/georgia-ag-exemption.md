# Georgia Agricultural Property Tax Exemption (Conservation Use Valuation) — Beekeeping Research

**Research Date:** February 9, 2026
**Purpose:** Build Georgia beekeeping agricultural exemption calculator

---

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.4 — Conservation Use Valuation Assessment (CUVA)

Georgia offers two main agricultural property tax programs:

1. **Conservation Use Valuation Assessment (CUVA)** — O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.4
   - Property assessed at **current use value** rather than fair market value
   - Requires a **10-year covenant** with the county Board of Tax Assessors
   - Most significant tax savings (often 80-95% reduction on land taxes)

2. **Preferential Agricultural Assessment** — O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.1
   - Property assessed at **75% of normal assessment** (30% of FMV instead of 40%)
   - Also requires a **10-year covenant**
   - Less savings than CUVA but fewer restrictions

### Georgia Property Tax Structure
- Georgia assesses property at **40% of Fair Market Value** (O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7)
- Under CUVA, land is assessed at **current use value** (agricultural productivity)
- Under Preferential Agricultural, land is assessed at **30% of FMV** (instead of 40%)
- Property tax rates set at county level (millage rates)
- Property tax returns due between January 1 and April 1

### Constitutional Basis
- Georgia Constitution, Article VII, Section I, Paragraph III authorizes the General Assembly to provide for conservation use assessment of bona fide agricultural property

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Does Beekeeping Qualify?

**YES** — Beekeeping qualifies under Georgia's CUVA program.

O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.4(a)(1) defines "bona fide conservation use" to include:
> "The production of agricultural products including but not limited to livestock, livestock products, poultry, poultry products, grain, hay, pastures, soybeans, cotton, tobacco, fruit, vegetables, and **other food or fiber products**..."

**Honey and bee products are agricultural products.** Georgia Department of Agriculture recognizes beekeeping as agriculture. The state has the **Georgia Beekeepers Association** and UGA Extension actively promotes apiculture.

### Key Requirements for CUVA with Beekeeping:
1. Property must be used for **bona fide conservation purposes**
2. Must enter a **10-year covenant** (binding agreement)
3. Owner must file application with the **County Board of Tax Assessors**
4. Land must be maintained in agricultural use for the full 10 years
5. Must demonstrate **good faith agricultural use** — not merely token placement of hives

### Georgia Beekeeping Registration
- Georgia Department of Agriculture requires **registration of apiaries**
- Georgia Bee Law: O.C.G.A. § 2-14-40 et seq.
- Annual inspection may be required
- Registration helps document agricultural use for CUVA

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State-Level Requirements

**CUVA (O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.4):**
- Minimum **10 acres** for bona fide agricultural or conservation use
- Exception: Property contiguous to qualifying property of same owner may qualify at smaller sizes
- The 10-acre minimum is a **state-level requirement**

**Preferential Agricultural (O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.1):**
- Minimum **10 acres** of bona fide agricultural property
- Similar 10-year covenant requirement

### County Variations
- Counties generally follow the state 10-acre minimum
- Some counties may have additional local requirements
- Board of Tax Assessors has discretion in individual cases

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Requirements
- Georgia law does **NOT specify a minimum number of hives**
- The requirement is "bona fide" agricultural use
- County assessors determine what constitutes bona fide use

### Practical Requirements
- **Typical expectation:** 1-2 hives per acre for smaller properties
- **10 acres:** Generally 6-12 hives expected
- **20+ acres:** 10-20 hives, or combination with other ag uses
- Must show **genuine agricultural operation**, not token placement
- Documentation of honey production, sales, and management helps

### Georgia Department of Agriculture Registration
- Register hives with the Georgia Department of Agriculture
- Get an apiary registration number
- This documentation strengthens CUVA application

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Filing a CUVA Application

1. **Where to Apply:** County Board of Tax Assessors office
2. **Form:** PT-283A (Application for Current Use Assessment)
3. **Deadline:** Must be filed between **January 1 and April 1** of the tax year
   - Some counties accept late applications with cause shown
4. **Documentation Needed:**
   - Completed PT-283A form
   - Legal description of property
   - Description of agricultural use (beekeeping operation details)
   - Number of hives and their locations
   - Georgia Department of Agriculture apiary registration
   - Evidence of honey production/sales
   - Farm plan or management plan
5. **10-Year Covenant:** Owner must sign a covenant agreeing to maintain agricultural use for 10 years

### Important Application Details
- The covenant **runs with the land** — if property is sold, new owner must continue agricultural use or face penalties
- All owners of the property must sign the covenant
- Property must be assessed on the January 1 tax date
- First-year applications require full documentation

### Renewal
- Once approved, the CUVA continues automatically during the 10-year covenant period
- No annual reapplication needed unless ownership changes
- County assessor may verify continued use at any time
- At end of 10-year period, must apply for new covenant

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### Typical Reduction

**CUVA Assessment:**
- Market value land: $5,000-$50,000/acre (varies widely)
- CUVA agricultural value: $150-$500/acre (based on productivity)
- **Savings: 85-99% reduction on land taxes**

**Example — 15 acres in Cherokee County (Metro Atlanta fringe):**
- Market value: $100,000/acre × 15 = $1,500,000
- Assessment at 40% = $600,000
- Tax at 25 mills = $15,000/year

- CUVA value: $300/acre × 15 = $4,500
- Assessment at 40% = $1,800
- Tax at 25 mills = $45/year
- **Annual savings: $14,955**

**Example — 20 acres in rural South Georgia (Berrien County):**
- Market value: $5,000/acre × 20 = $100,000
- Assessment at 40% = $40,000
- Tax at 20 mills = $800/year

- CUVA value: $200/acre × 20 = $4,000
- Assessment at 40% = $1,600
- Tax at 20 mills = $32/year
- **Annual savings: $768**

### Preferential Agricultural Assessment (Alternative)
- Less savings: property assessed at 30% of FMV instead of 40%
- 25% reduction in assessed value
- Useful for properties that may not qualify for full CUVA

## 7. COUNTY STRUCTURE

### Overview
- **Georgia has 159 counties** (second most in the US after Texas)
- Property assessment by **County Board of Tax Assessors**
- Tax collection by **County Tax Commissioner** (or Tax Collector)
- Each county has its own Board of Tax Assessors that reviews CUVA applications

### Key Officials
- **Board of Tax Assessors** — Determines property values, reviews CUVA applications
- **Tax Commissioner** — Collects taxes, issues tax bills
- **Board of Equalization** — Handles appeals

### Administration
- Applications filed with Tax Assessors office
- County boards have discretion on approving CUVA
- State Department of Revenue provides oversight and guidelines
- Georgia County Tax Assessors' website: varies by county

## 9. HONEY PRODUCTION

### Georgia Climate & Beekeeping

**Georgia Climate Zones:**
- **North Georgia Mountains:** USDA Zones 6b-7b; seasonal beekeeping, good spring flows
- **Piedmont (Central):** USDA Zones 7b-8a; excellent year-round potential
- **Coastal Plain (South):** USDA Zones 8a-9a; long season, multiple nectar flows

**Average Honey Production:**
- **North Georgia:** 40-60 lbs/hive/year
- **Central Georgia:** 50-80 lbs/hive/year
- **South Georgia / Coastal:** 60-100 lbs/hive/year
- **State average:** ~55-70 lbs/hive/year

**Major Nectar Sources in Georgia:**
- Spring: Tulip poplar, black locust, clover, fruit trees
- Summer: Sourwood (prized, North Georgia), wildflowers, cotton
- Fall: Goldenrod, aster
- **Sourwood honey** from North Georgia mountains is among the most valuable US honeys ($15-25/lb retail)

**Georgia Honey Industry:**
- Georgia ranks in the top 15 states for honey production
- Approximately 4,500+ beekeepers in the state
- Georgia Beekeepers Association (GBA) is very active
- UGA has an active apiculture extension program

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Breaking the CUVA Covenant

**If agricultural use is discontinued during the 10-year covenant (O.C.G.A. § 48-5-7.4):**

1. **Rollback taxes:** Difference between CUVA tax and what would have been owed at fair market value, for **all years the covenant was in effect** (up to 10 years)
2. **Penalty:** Additional **20% of the rollback tax amount** as a penalty for early breach
3. **Interest:** Interest on the rollback tax amount

**Breach Triggers:**
- Conversion of land to non-agricultural use
- Sale of property for non-agricultural purposes
- Development or subdivision of the land
- Failure to maintain bona fide agricultural use
- Owner's voluntary withdrawal from covenant

**Exceptions (No Penalty):**
- Condemnation/eminent domain (involuntary conversion)
- Transfer between qualifying family members who continue ag use
- Death of owner (heirs may continue covenant)
- Certain government-required changes

### Financial Impact of Breach
**Example:** 15 acres in Gwinnett County, CUVA for 7 years
- Total tax savings over 7 years: ~$70,000
- Rollback taxes: ~$70,000
- 20% penalty: ~$14,000
- Interest: ~$5,000
- **Total penalty: ~$89,000**

**This is a serious financial commitment.** Property owners should be certain of their 10-year commitment.

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Can You Have Both Homestead + CUVA?

**YES** — Georgia allows both a homestead exemption and CUVA on the same property.

**How It Works:**
- **Homestead exemption** applies to the **residence and underlying land** (typically 1 acre)
- **CUVA** applies to the **remaining agricultural land**
- They are separate exemptions/assessments that can coexist

**Georgia Homestead Exemptions:**
- **Standard:** $2,000 exemption from assessed value (state level)
- **Age 65+:** $4,000 exemption (income limits apply)
- **Age 62+:** Additional school tax exemption
- **Local homestead exemptions** vary widely by county (many are more generous than state)
- **Floating inflation-proof exemption** for age 62+ (income limits)

**Optimal Strategy:**
1. Apply for homestead exemption on the residence + immediate land
2. Apply for CUVA on remaining agricultural acreage (10+ acres)
3. Maintain beekeeping operation on CUVA land
4. Result: Reduced taxes on both residence and agricultural land

**Important Notes:**
- Residence/home is still taxed at market value (less homestead exemption)
- CUVA only reduces land taxes, not improvement taxes
- Must apply separately for each — homestead with Tax Commissioner, CUVA with Board of Tax Assessors

## 13. ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Georgia-Specific Nuances

1. **10-Year Covenant is Binding:** Unlike Florida (automatic classification) or Texas (annual), Georgia requires a 10-year commitment. Breaking it triggers significant penalties.

2. **All Owners Must Sign:** If property has multiple owners, all must sign the CUVA covenant.

3. **Covenant Runs with the Land:** New owners assume the covenant obligations upon purchase.

4. **Family Transfer Exception:** Transfers between qualifying family members (spouse, children, parents, siblings) may continue the covenant without restart.

5. **Death of Owner:** Heirs can continue the covenant, but must file within 12 months of probate.

6. **Partial Breach:** If only part of the property changes use, rollback may apply only to that portion (county discretion).

7. **Forestry Combined Use:** Beekeeping pairs well with forestry (timber) on Georgia properties — both qualify for CUVA, and bees benefit from forest flowers.
