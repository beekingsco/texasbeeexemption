## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statute: N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.1 through 54:4-23.23 — Farmland Assessment Act of 1964
**Source:** New Jersey Statutes Annotated, Title 54, Chapter 4

New Jersey's agricultural property tax program is called **"Farmland Assessment"** (sometimes called "Farm Assessment" or "FA-1"). It allows qualifying farmland to be assessed based on its **agricultural or horticultural use value** rather than its true market value. Given New Jersey's extremely high property tax rates (highest in the nation), this program provides **enormous tax savings**.

### Key Code Sections:
- **N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.1** — Short title; definitions
- **N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.2** — Qualification requirements
- **N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.3** — Application procedures
- **N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.4** — Assessment methodology
- **N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.5** — Denial/appeal procedures
- **N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.6** — Rollback taxes upon disqualification
- **N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.14** — Beekeeping specifically (2015 amendment)

### Administering Agencies:
- **Local Tax Assessor** — Each municipality administers farmland assessment
- **County Tax Board** — Appeals go here
- **NJ Division of Taxation** — Sets farmland assessment values statewide
- **NJ State Farmland Evaluation Advisory Committee** — Annual guidelines

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Is Beekeeping Explicitly Recognized?
**YES — New Jersey explicitly added beekeeping in 2015 legislation.**

**N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.14** (added by P.L. 2015, c.72):
- Specifically provides that land **actively devoted to the production of honey and other apiculture products** qualifies for farmland assessment
- This was a landmark addition that removed ambiguity about beekeeping qualification

### Key Beekeeping Rules:
- Land must be **actively devoted** to beekeeping for commercial purposes
- Beekeeping includes honey production, beeswax, pollination services, queen rearing, nucleus colony production
- Must meet the same general farmland assessment requirements (acreage, income, etc.)
- **Hobby beekeeping does NOT qualify** — must be a bona fide agricultural operation

### 2015 Bee Law (P.L. 2015, c.72):
- Popularly known as the "Bee Bill"
- Specifically stated that apiaries and beekeeping operations are agricultural/horticultural activities
- Resolved prior uncertainty about whether beekeeping alone could qualify land for farmland assessment

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State-Mandated Minimums (N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.2):

#### Minimum Land Area:
- **5 acres minimum** for the first farmland assessment
- The 5 acres must have been **actively devoted to agricultural or horticultural use** for at least the **2 successive years preceding the tax year**
- Additional land can qualify if it's part of the same operation

#### Exception — Less Than 5 Acres:
- Land of **less than 5 acres** can qualify if it produces a **gross income of at least $500** from the sale of agricultural/horticultural products
- AND the land has been devoted to such use for the preceding 2 years
- This is particularly relevant for beekeeping operations

#### Woodland/Wetland/Cropland Mix:
- Land devoted to agriculture (including beekeeping): **5 acres minimum**
- Appurtenant woodland/wetlands: Can be included if part of the farm and contiguous
- Maximum woodland acreage: No fixed limit, but must be part of an active farm operation

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### No State-Mandated Minimum Hive Count in Statute
New Jersey does not specify a specific number of hives in the Farmland Assessment Act.

### Practical Requirements:
- Must meet the **$500 minimum annual gross income** requirement (for parcels under 5 acres) OR the **$500 minimum for the first 5 acres plus $5 per additional acre** requirement
- Income requirement for standard farmland: **$500 for first 5 acres + $5 per additional acre per year**
- Example: 10-acre parcel needs $525/year ($500 + 5×$5)
- Average NJ honey production: **40-60 lbs/hive/year**
- At $10-15/lb retail, each hive produces $400-900/year
- **Practical minimum: 2-4 hives** to meet income requirements
- Most assessors recommend **at least 4-6 hives** for credibility

### Documentation:
- Sales receipts for honey, wax, and other products
- Records of pollination service income
- Beekeeper registration with NJ Department of Agriculture
- Hive inspection records

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Where to Apply:
- **Municipal Tax Assessor's Office** — File with the tax assessor of the municipality where the land is located (NOT the county)
- New Jersey has 564 municipalities across 21 counties, each with their own assessor

### When to Apply:
- **Initial application deadline: August 1** of the pre-tax year
- For farmland assessment beginning in tax year 2027, apply by **August 1, 2026**
- Annual renewal: File annually (typically by August 1)

### Application Form:
- **Form FA-1** (Farmland Assessment Application)
- Also called the "Initial Application for Farmland Assessment"
- **Form FA-1-G** for renewal/continuation

### Required Documentation:
1. Completed FA-1 form
2. Proof of agricultural use for at least 2 preceding years
3. Income records (Schedule F, sales receipts, 1099s)
4. Description of agricultural activity
5. Evidence of hive registration (for beekeeping)
6. Parcel information and legal description

### Approval Process:
1. File with municipal tax assessor by August 1
2. Assessor reviews application
3. Assessor may conduct site visit
4. Approval or denial by October 1 (for following tax year)
5. If denied, appeal to County Tax Board within 45 days

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### New Jersey's Property Tax Crisis:
- NJ has the **highest property taxes in the nation**
- State average effective rate: **~2.23%** (2024)
- Some municipalities exceed **3-4%** effective rate
- Average residential property tax bill: **~$9,500/year** (highest in US)

### Farmland Assessment Values:
The NJ Division of Taxation publishes annual farmland assessment values:
- **Cropland Harvested:** $180-350/acre (vs. market value of $15,000-100,000+/acre)
- **Cropland Not Harvested:** $30-100/acre
- **Permanent Pasture:** $30-75/acre
- **Appurtenant Woodland:** $20-75/acre
- **Wetlands:** $4-20/acre
- **Beekeeping/Apiary Land:** Typically assessed similar to cropland ($150-350/acre)

- New Jersey has a moderate climate with good floral diversity

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY (N.J.S.A. 54:4-23.8)

### Rollback Taxes:
When land loses farmland assessment, **rollback taxes** are imposed:
- Calculated as the **difference between taxes paid under farmland assessment and what would have been paid at full market value**
- Applied for the current year PLUS the **2 preceding tax years** (total of 3 years)
- Interest accrues on rollback amounts

### When Rollback Is Triggered:
1. **Change of use** — Land converted to non-agricultural use
2. **Failure to meet requirements** — Income or use criteria not met
3. **Application not filed** — Failure to file annual continuation
4. **Development** — Land developed for residential, commercial, or industrial use
5. **Sale to non-farmer** — If new owner doesn't continue agricultural use

### Rollback Calculation Example:
- 10-acre property in Hunterdon County
- Market value: $80,000/acre ($800,000 total)
- Farmland assessment: $250/acre ($2,500 total)
- Tax rate: 2.20%
- Annual tax at market value: $17,600
- Annual tax at farmland assessment: $55
- Annual difference: $17,545
- **3-year rollback: ~$52,635 + interest**

### Exceptions to Rollback:
- Death of the owner (temporary, while estate settles)
- Condemnation/eminent domain
- Transfer to a qualifying farmer who continues agricultural use
- Municipal purchase for recreation/conservation

### Rollback Is a Lien:
- Constitutes a lien on the property
- Must be satisfied before transfer in many cases

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### New Jersey Homestead Benefit / ANCHOR Program:
- NJ replaced the Homestead Rebate with the **ANCHOR** (Affordable NJ Communities for Homeowners and Renters) program
- Provides property tax relief based on income:
  - Income under $150,000: $1,500 benefit
  - Income $150,000-$250,000: $1,000 benefit

### Can You Have Both Farmland Assessment + ANCHOR?
- **Yes** — They are completely separate programs
- Farmland Assessment reduces the **assessed value** of agricultural land
- ANCHOR provides a **credit/rebate** based on total property tax paid
- No conflict between the two
- The farmland assessment dramatically reduces the tax bill; ANCHOR may provide additional relief on the remaining tax

### Senior Freeze (Property Tax Reimbursement):
- Seniors 65+ can freeze their property tax amount
- Can be combined with farmland assessment
- The frozen amount would be based on the already-reduced farmland-assessed tax

### Practical Impact:
- NJ has the highest property taxes in the US, making farmland assessment **extraordinarily valuable**
- A typical NJ homeowner pays ~$9,500/year in property taxes
- Farmland assessment can reduce a 10-acre property's tax from thousands to under $100
- ANCHOR adds an additional $1,000-1,500 on top

## 13. ADDITIONAL NOTES

### NJ's Unique Position:
- **Highest property taxes in the US** — farmland assessment provides the biggest percentage savings of any state
- NJ is called the "Garden State" for a reason — agriculture is a $1.4 billion industry
- Proximity to NYC and Philadelphia creates enormous land value pressures
- Farmland assessment is critical to keeping farming viable in NJ

### Right to Farm Act:
- NJ's Right to Farm Act (N.J.S.A. 4:1C-1 et seq.) protects qualifying farms from nuisance complaints
- Farms with farmland assessment receive additional protections
- This is relevant for beekeeping, which can face neighbor complaints

### Transfer of Development Rights (TDR):
- NJ has TDR programs in some counties (notably Burlington, Monmouth)
- Farmland preservation through development rights purchase
- Can work alongside farmland assessment

### Minimum Income Thresholds Are Low:
- **$500 + $5/acre over 5 acres** is very achievable with beekeeping
- A 10-acre property only needs $525/year in gross ag income
- Even 2-3 productive hives can meet this threshold
- This makes NJ's program very accessible for small-scale beekeepers

### Municipal Administration:
- Unlike other states where the county assessor handles everything, NJ uses **municipal tax assessors**
- This means rules and interpretation can vary from town to town within the same county
- Always check with the specific municipality's tax assessor
