## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Law: Arkansas Constitution Article 16, §5 and Amendment 59
**Also known as:** Agricultural land assessment at "use value" rather than market value

#### Constitutional Foundation:
- **Arkansas Constitution Article 16, §5** — Establishes that all real and personal property shall be assessed for taxation
- **Amendment 59 (1980)** — Grants the General Assembly authority to provide for agricultural, pasture, and timber land to be assessed at use value for property tax purposes

#### Implementing Statutes:
- **ACA §26-26-407** — Agricultural land valuation; defines "agricultural land" for property tax purposes
- **ACA §26-26-408** — Methods for determining agricultural use value
- **ACA §26-26-1201 through §26-26-1209** — Detailed provisions for agricultural land assessment
- **ACA §2-7-101 through §2-7-113** — Arkansas Beekeeping Regulation Act

### Assessment Coordination Division (ACD):
The Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration, Assessment Coordination Division, oversees property tax assessment statewide and provides guidelines for agricultural use value calculation.

### Key Points:
- Agricultural land is assessed at **20% of its use value** (the standard assessment rate for all real property)
- Use value is determined by agricultural income capitalization methodology
- The difference between market value and use value creates the tax benefit
- The ACD publishes annual agricultural use value tables by soil type and capability class

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### ✅ Beekeeping Qualifies as Agricultural Use in Arkansas

Arkansas has a **strong beekeeping tradition** and explicitly recognizes apiculture as agriculture.

#### Legal Recognition:
- **ACA §2-7-101** — Arkansas Beekeeping Regulation Act: defines beekeeping as an agricultural activity
- **ACA §26-26-407** — "Agricultural land" includes land used for producing "any product of the soil, livestock, poultry, or dairy products" — bees and honey qualify under "livestock" and agricultural products
- The Arkansas Plant Board (now part of the Arkansas Agriculture Department) regulates beekeeping

#### Beekeeping-Specific Rules:
- **Registration:** All beekeepers must register with the Arkansas Agriculture Department (formerly State Plant Board)
- **Registration is FREE** — no fee to register hives
- **Inspection:** State apiary inspectors may inspect hives for disease
- Arkansas has active beekeeping organizations: Arkansas Beekeepers Association, various county/regional clubs

### Requirements for Agricultural Classification:
1. Land must be **primarily used** for agricultural production
2. Must demonstrate **bona fide agricultural activity** (not merely holding land)
3. Income from agricultural production OR evidence of active farming/ranching
4. The county assessor evaluates based on actual use

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State Level: No Statutory Minimum Acreage
Arkansas law does not specify a minimum acreage for agricultural use value assessment. The focus is on **bona fide agricultural use** rather than a specific size.

### Practical Standards:
- **Most counties:** Assessors generally look for at least 5-10 acres to grant agricultural classification
- **Some counties:** May accept smaller tracts (3-5 acres) if the applicant demonstrates genuine commercial agricultural activity
- **Urban-adjacent counties (Pulaski, Benton, Washington):** May have higher practical thresholds (10+ acres)
- **Rural counties:** More lenient — some accept any tract with documented agricultural production

### Recommended for Beekeeping:
- **5+ acres** is a safe minimum for most Arkansas counties
- **10+ acres** provides a strong basis in more urban counties
- Smaller tracts can qualify with strong documentation of commercial beekeeping

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Level: No Statutory Minimum Hive Count
Arkansas does not specify a minimum number of hives for agricultural classification.

### Practical Standards:
- Most county assessors expect evidence of **genuine commercial activity**
- A general guideline of **5-10 hives** minimum for agricultural classification
- Having more hives relative to acreage strengthens the application
- Registration with the Arkansas Agriculture Department adds credibility
- Maintaining sales records or Schedule F documentation is important

### Arkansas Beekeeping Context:
- Arkansas is a significant beekeeping state (historically one of the leading states for commercial beekeeping)
- The state has both large commercial operations and many small-scale beekeepers
- County assessors are generally familiar with beekeeping as agricultural activity

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### How to Apply:
1. **Contact the county assessor's office** — Arkansas has 75 counties, each with an elected County Assessor
2. **Submit an agricultural use application** — Request that your property be assessed at agricultural use value
3. **Provide supporting documentation:**
   - Arkansas Agriculture Department beekeeping registration
   - Hive inventory and location information
   - Honey production records and sales records
   - Farm income documentation (Schedule F from federal taxes)
   - Business plan or farm plan
4. **Assessor review** — The assessor may conduct a site visit to verify agricultural use

### Assessment Cycle:
- Arkansas uses a **reassessment cycle** — all property is reappraised every 5 years (previously 3 years, changed to 5 years by Act 1185 of 2013)
- Between reassessments, agricultural classification continues unless use changes
- New construction or land use changes trigger revaluation

### Who Administers:
- **County Assessor** — Elected official in each of 75 counties; responsible for property valuation
- **Assessment Coordination Division (ACD)** — State oversight body under Department of Finance and Administration; publishes use value guidelines
- **County Equalization Board** — Hears assessment appeals at county level
- **Arkansas Tax Appeals Commission** — State-level appeal body

### Deadlines:
- **Assessment date:** January 1 of each year (lien date)
- **Application:** Should be filed with the county assessor before the assessment rolls are prepared (typically by spring)
- **Appeal deadline:** Varies by county; typically 20 days after notice of assessment
- **Tax bills:** Due October 15; delinquent after that date
- **Recommended:** Apply or verify classification by **February-March** each year

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How Agricultural Use Value Creates Savings

Arkansas assesses all real property at **20% of assessed value** (which equals market value or use value, as applicable). The millage rate is then applied.

| Factor | Market Value Assessment | Agricultural Use Value |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Land Value | $3,000-$15,000/acre (varies) | $100-$600/acre (use value) |
| Assessment Rate | 20% of market value | 20% of use value |
| Assessed Value (10 acres @ $5,000/acre) | $10,000 | $400-$1,200 |

### Example Calculation (10-acre rural tract):
- **Fair Market Value:** $5,000/acre × 10 acres = $50,000
- **Assessed at FMV:** $50,000 × 20% = $10,000 assessed value
- **Agricultural Use Value:** $300/acre × 10 acres = $3,000
- **Assessed at Use Value:** $3,000 × 20% = $600 assessed value
- **Total millage rate (example):** 50 mills (typical rural county)
- **Tax at FMV:** $10,000 × 0.050 = **$500/year**
- **Tax at Use Value:** $600 × 0.050 = **$30/year**
- **Annual Savings: ~$470 (94% reduction)**

### Typical Tax Savings Range:
- **Rural counties:** 70-90% reduction in property taxes on land
- **Suburban counties:** 85-95% reduction
- **Near urban areas (NW Arkansas, Little Rock):** 90-97% reduction (larger gap between market and use value)

### Important Notes:
- Arkansas already has relatively **low property taxes** compared to national averages
- The homestead property tax credit (Amendment 79) provides additional relief on primary residences
- Combined with agricultural use value, total tax bills can be very modest

## 8. AVERAGE TAX RATES

### Arkansas Property Tax System:
- Arkansas uses **millage rates** (mills per dollar of assessed value)
- All real property is assessed at **20% of market value** (or 20% of use value for agricultural land)
- Millage rates vary significantly by county and taxing district (schools, roads, fire, etc.)

### State Millage: 3.0 mills (state general purposes)

### Typical Total Millage Rates by County:
- **Statewide average:** approximately 45-55 mills (combining all levies)
- **Rural counties:** 35-50 mills
- **Urban counties:** 50-70 mills
- **Notable counties:**
  - Pulaski County (Little Rock): ~60-65 mills
  - Benton County (NW Arkansas): ~45-55 mills
  - Washington County (Fayetteville): ~50-55 mills
  - Craighead County (Jonesboro): ~50-55 mills
  - Garland County (Hot Springs): ~45-55 mills

### Effective Tax Rates (as % of fair market value):
- **Average effective rate on land:** ~0.6-1.0% of fair market value
- **Average effective rate on ag land (use value):** ~0.03-0.10% of fair market value
- **Arkansas ranks among the lowest property tax states** in the US

### Amendment 79 (2000):
- Caps property tax increases at 5% per year for homestead property, 10% per year for non-homestead
- This constitutional amendment provides additional protection against rapid tax increases
- Does NOT apply to new construction or improvements

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Arkansas's Approach to Agricultural Use Value Changes

**Arkansas has a rollback tax provision**, though it is less severe than some states.

#### What happens when agricultural use ends:
1. **Reclassification:** The assessor reclassifies the property from agricultural use value to fair market value
2. **Rollback period:** The property owner may owe rollback taxes for the **previous 5 years** (the difference between what was paid under use value and what would have been owed under market value)
3. **Application:** The rollback applies when land is converted from agricultural use to a non-agricultural use (e.g., development, subdivision)

### Key Details:
- **Rollback period:** Up to 5 years of back taxes (the difference between use value and market value assessments)
- **Triggers:** Sale or conversion of agricultural land to non-agricultural use
- **Exceptions:** Transfer by inheritance, involuntary conversion (eminent domain), or transfer between family members generally does NOT trigger rollback
- **Interest:** Interest may be charged on rollback amounts

### Comparison to Other States:
- **Texas:** 5-year rollback + 7% interest (very aggressive)
- **Arkansas:** Up to 5-year rollback (less aggressive than Texas but still significant)
- **Louisiana:** No rollback at all (most favorable)
- **Florida:** No formal rollback for ag classification

### Practical Impact for BeeKings Customers:
- Customers should understand the rollback provision before committing to agricultural use value
- For long-term landowners who plan to maintain agricultural use, the rollback is not a concern
- The rollback primarily affects developers who use ag classification temporarily to reduce taxes

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### ✅ Yes — Homestead Tax Credit + Agricultural Use Value Can BOTH Apply

#### Amendment 79 (2000) — Property Tax Relief:
- Provides a **homestead property tax credit** of up to $375 per year on the primary residence
- This credit applies to the residence and up to 1 acre of land
- Agricultural use value applies to the remaining agricultural land

#### How They Work Together:
1. **Agricultural Use Value:** Land beyond the homestead is assessed at 20% of use value
2. **Homestead Credit:** Primary residence receives up to $375 annual credit against property taxes
3. **5% Cap:** Annual increases in assessed value on homestead property capped at 5% per year
4. **Combined effect:** Very low total property tax burden

### Example (Rural Homestead with Beekeeping):
- **10 acres with house:**
  - Home + 1 acre assessed at market: $150,000 × 20% = $30,000 assessed value
  - Remaining 9 acres at ag use value: $300/acre × 9 = $2,700 × 20% = $540 assessed value
  - Total assessed value: $30,540
  - Total tax (at 50 mills): $30,540 × 0.050 = $1,527
  - Homestead credit: -$375
  - **Net annual tax: $1,152**
  - Without ag use value: $50,000 FMV × 20% = $10,000 + $30,000 = $40,000 × 0.050 = $2,000 - $375 = **$1,625**
  - **Savings from ag use value: ~$473/year**

## 13. ADDITIONAL NOTES FOR BEEKINGS

### Why Arkansas is Excellent for BeeKings Expansion:
1. **Proximity to Canton, TX:** Arkansas borders Texas — east Texas to western Arkansas is just 2-4 hours
2. **Strong beekeeping tradition:** Arkansas assessors understand and respect beekeeping as agriculture
3. **Low property taxes:** Already one of the lowest-tax states in the US
4. **Diverse forage:** Ozarks, delta, and piedmont provide varied and excellent nectar sources
5. **University support:** University of Arkansas has an active apiculture extension program
6. **Growing market:** NW Arkansas (Walmart/Tyson corridor) is one of the fastest-growing regions in the US — high demand for local honey
7. **Amendment 79 protections:** Tax increases are capped, providing long-term stability

### Potential Challenges:
1. **5-year rollback:** Unlike Louisiana, Arkansas does have rollback provisions
2. **Assessment variation:** Each county assessor operates somewhat independently
3. **Limited online resources:** Some rural county assessor offices have minimal web presence
4. **Weather:** Ozark winters can be harsh for bees; proper winterization needed in northern counties
5. **SHB & varroa:** Small hive beetle pressure is moderate; varroa management is essential

### Arkansas Agriculture Department Registration:
- All beekeepers should register hives with the Arkansas Agriculture Department
- Registration is **FREE**
- Provides credibility for agricultural use value applications
- Contact: Arkansas Agriculture Department, Plant Industries Division
- Phone: (501) 225-1598
- Website: https://www.agriculture.arkansas.gov

### Key Markets for Honey:
- **NW Arkansas:** Bentonville, Fayetteville, Rogers — affluent market for artisan/local honey
- **Little Rock metro:** Central Arkansas population center
- **Hot Springs:** Tourist area with strong demand for local products
- **Memphis corridor (NE Arkansas):** Jonesboro, West Memphis — growing market
