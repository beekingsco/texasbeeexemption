## ✅ CONFIRMED FINDINGS

### TEXAS (TIER 1 - CONFIRMED LAUNCH-READY)
- ✅ Agricultural use valuation: **YES**
- ✅ Beekeeping qualifies: **EXPLICITLY LISTED**
- ✅ Statute: Texas Tax Code Chapter 23, Subchapter D
- ✅ Min acreage: 5-20 acres (varies by county)
- ✅ Min hives: ~6 hives per qualifying acreage
- ✅ Deadline: January 1 - April 30
- ✅ Administrator: County Appraisal Districts
- ✅ Savings: **HIGH** (avg tax rate 1.8%, ag rate $50-200/acre)
- ✅ Registration: None required for exemption

**Status: READY FOR IMMEDIATE LAUNCH**

### CALIFORNIA (TIER 2/3 - PARTIALLY CONFIRMED)
- ✅ Agricultural use valuation: **YES** (Williamson Act since 1965)
- ⚠️ Beekeeping qualifies: **LIKELY** (broad ag definition, county-specific)
- ✅ Statute: California Land Conservation Act of 1965
- ⚠️ Min acreage: Set by each county (typically 10-100+ acres)
- ❓ Min hives: Not specified at state level
- ⚠️ Deadline: Varies by county
- ✅ Administrator: County governments (highly decentralized)
- ✅ Savings: **HIGH** (taxes based on ag income, not market value)
- ⚠️ Registration: Varies by county

**Status: REQUIRES COUNTY-BY-COUNTY RESEARCH**

## 🎓 LESSONS LEARNED

### What Worked:
- Framework approach allows systematic research
- Phone methodology is necessary given web limitations
- Extension offices often more helpful than state bureaucracies
- Beekeeping associations are valuable resources

### What Didn't Work:
- Direct web_fetch of state websites (too many broken URLs)
- Assuming uniform URL patterns across states
- Expecting one-size-fits-all programs

### Recommendations for Future:
- Always have phone research as backup
- Build relationships with state contacts early
- Allow more time than expected for government responsiveness
- Document everything - requirements change
