# Ohio CAUV (Current Agricultural Use Value) for Beekeeping - Complete Research

**Research Date:** February 9, 2026  
**Purpose:** Build Ohio beekeeping agricultural exemption calculator

## 1. LEGAL BASIS

### Primary Statutes: Ohio Revised Code §5713.30 - §5713.37
**Also known as:** "CAUV" — Current Agricultural Use Value

#### Key Sections:
- **§5713.30** — Definitions (including "land devoted exclusively to agricultural use")
- **§5713.31** — Application procedures and valuation process
- **§5713.34** — Recoupment (rollback) charges on conversion
- **§5713.35** — Additional recoupment provisions
- **§929.02** — Agricultural district provisions (alternative path)

#### Constitutional Basis:
- **Ohio Constitution, Article II, Section 36** — Authorizes the General Assembly to provide by law for the assessment of agricultural land at current agricultural use value

### How CAUV Works:
Instead of being taxed on fair market value, qualifying agricultural land is taxed on its **Current Agricultural Use Value (CAUV)** — calculated based on soil types and their agricultural productivity. The Ohio Department of Taxation publishes CAUV values per acre for each soil type in the state.

## 2. BEEKEEPING QUALIFICATION

### Explicit Recognition — YES ✅
Per ORC §5713.30(A)(1)(a), land devoted exclusively to agricultural use includes:
> "**apiculture**" (beekeeping)

This is explicitly listed alongside:
- Commercial animal or poultry husbandry
- Aquaculture, algaculture
- Hemp cultivation
- Field crops, tobacco, fruits, vegetables, nursery stock, etc.

### Two Size Categories:

#### Category 1: 10+ Acres (§5713.30(A)(1))
- Land totaling **not less than 10 acres**
- Must have been devoted exclusively to agricultural use (including apiculture) for **3 calendar years prior** to application
- No minimum income requirement

#### Category 2: Under 10 Acres (§5713.30(A)(2))
- Land totaling **less than 10 acres**
- Must have been devoted exclusively to apiculture for 3 prior years
- **Must produce average yearly gross income of at least $2,500** during the 3-year period OR show evidence of anticipated gross income of that amount
- This is the key threshold for small-acreage beekeepers

---

## 3. MINIMUM ACREAGE

### State Requirements:
- **No minimum acreage** if gross income exceeds $2,500/year average
- **10 acres:** Qualifies without income requirement if devoted to agriculture for 3 years
- **Under 10 acres:** Must meet the $2,500 gross income threshold

### Practical Impact for Beekeepers:
- A beekeeper with 20+ hives on 2 acres producing $2,500+ annually qualifies
- A beekeeper with 50 hives on 15 acres qualifies regardless of income
- The 3-year prior use requirement means new beekeepers must wait before applying (or show anticipated income)

---

## 4. MINIMUM HIVES

### State Requirements:
- **No minimum hive count** in state law
- Must demonstrate "exclusive" agricultural use
- For under 10 acres: must show $2,500/year gross income

### Practical Hive Estimates to Meet Income Threshold:
- At 60 lbs/hive × $10/lb = $600/hive/year
- **$2,500 ÷ $600 = approximately 5 hives minimum** to meet income requirement
- Recommended: **8-12 hives** to comfortably exceed the threshold and account for bad years

## 5. APPLICATION PROCESS

### Filing Period:
- **After the first Monday in January** through the **first Monday in March** (annual)
- Late applications: if previous year's CAUV land not renewed, auditor notifies owner; owner has until **first Monday in April** to file

### Where to Apply:
- Filed with the **County Auditor** of the county where land is located

### Application Types:
1. **Initial application:** First-time filing, requires $25 fee
2. **Renewal application:** Filed in subsequent years, no fee, requires only minimal information to confirm continued eligibility

### Annual Requirement:
- **CAUV requires annual renewal** (unlike some other states)
- Failure to renew = loss of CAUV status + rollback charges
- Renewal is simplified but must be filed during the filing window

### Process:
1. File application with county auditor during Jan-March window
2. Auditor reviews for completeness and correctness
3. Auditor views (inspects) land prior to first Monday in August
4. If land qualifies, CAUV valuation is applied for current tax year
5. CAUV values are based on soil types per Ohio Dept of Taxation tables

## 6. TAX SAVINGS

### How CAUV Valuation Works:
- Ohio Dept of Taxation publishes per-acre CAUV values for each soil type
- County auditor applies these values based on actual soil types on the property
- **Soil type determines value** — not just flat per-acre rates

### Typical CAUV Values vs. Market Values:
| Soil Quality | CAUV Value/Acre | Market Value/Acre | Savings |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|---------|
| Prime farmland | $1,200-$2,200 | $8,000-$15,000 | 75-90% |
| Average cropland | $600-$1,200 | $5,000-$10,000 | 80-90% |
| Pasture/hay | $200-$600 | $3,000-$8,000 | 85-95% |
| Woodland | $50-$200 | $2,000-$6,000 | 90-97% |

### Example Savings (10 acres, average soil):
| Factor | Market Value | CAUV Value |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| Land value (10 acres) | $80,000 | $8,000 |
| Tax rate (75 mills effective) | $6,000/year | $600/year |
| **Annual savings** | — | **$5,400** |

*Ohio's property tax rates are relatively high, making CAUV savings very significant*

### Recent CAUV Value Trends:
- CAUV values increased significantly 2022-2025 due to higher crop prices
- Still dramatically below market values
- Recalculated based on 10-year Olympic average of crop yields and prices

## 7. COUNTY STRUCTURE

### Overview:
- Ohio has **88 counties**
- Property tax is administered by the **County Auditor**
- The auditor handles assessment, CAUV applications, and maintains property records

### Key Offices:
- **County Auditor** — Primary office for CAUV applications, property assessment
- **County Treasurer** — Collects property taxes
- **Board of Revision** — Hears appeals on property valuation

### Assessment Cycle:
- Ohio requires counties to conduct reappraisals every **6 years**
- Triennial updates in between
- CAUV values are recalculated annually by the state

## 11. ROLLBACK / PENALTY

### Recoupment Charges (§5713.34):
When agricultural land is converted from CAUV to non-agricultural use:

#### Charge Amount:
- Equal to the **tax savings for the 3 most recent tax years** preceding conversion
- Tax savings = difference between what was paid under CAUV and what would have been paid at fair market value

### What Triggers Conversion (§5713.30(B)):
1. Failure to file renewal without good cause
2. Failure of new owner to file initial application without good cause  
3. Land no longer qualifies as exclusively agricultural
4. Land lies idle/fallow for more than 1 year (unless good cause shown to Board of Revision — up to 3 years)
5. Action inconsistent with agricultural use

### Exception:
- Public entities and eminent domain acquisitions may have different rules
- Energy facility construction on a portion of agricultural land is exempt from recoupment if remaining portion stays agricultural

### Key Protections:
- **Board of Revision** determines "good cause" for missed filings
- Land can lie fallow up to 1 year without losing status
- With good cause, up to 3 years fallow is allowed
- Agritourism does NOT disqualify land from CAUV

## 12. HOMESTEAD INTERACTION

### Can You Have Both? **YES**

#### Ohio Homestead Exemption:
- Available to homeowners age 65+ OR permanently disabled
- **Income limit:** Modified Adjusted Gross Income ≤ $36,100 (as of 2024, indexed annually)
- **Exemption amount:** Reduces assessed value by $26,200 (saves approximately $400-$600/year depending on district)
- Enhanced homestead exemption available for qualifying low-income seniors

#### Interaction with CAUV:
- **Homestead exemption applies to the dwelling/homesite**
- **CAUV applies to the agricultural land**
- Both can apply to the same property simultaneously
- The home/yard is assessed at market value (with homestead exemption)
- The agricultural portion is assessed at CAUV

#### Practical Application:
1. Home + yard = market value assessment with homestead exemption (if qualifying)
2. Agricultural land (beekeeping areas) = CAUV assessment
3. Maximum tax savings achieved by combining both programs

## ADDITIONAL NOTES

### Ohio-Specific Considerations:
1. **CAUV values are soil-type based** — the Ohio Dept of Taxation publishes values for every soil type in the state, not just flat per-acre rates
2. **Annual renewal is mandatory** — unlike Florida or Alabama, Ohio requires annual applications
3. **$2,500 income threshold** for under 10 acres is critical for beekeepers to track
4. **Apiculture is explicitly named** in the statute — no ambiguity about beekeeping qualifying
5. **Ohio has very high property taxes** — CAUV savings are substantial (often $500-$5,000+ annually)
6. **Agricultural districts** (ORC §929.02) provide an alternative path with additional protections against annexation and zoning changes

### Ohio Apiary Registration:
- Ohio requires registration of apiaries with the Ohio Department of Agriculture
- Registration is annual and provides inspection services
- **ODA Apiary Program** phone: (614) 728-6373
- Registration strengthens CAUV applications
